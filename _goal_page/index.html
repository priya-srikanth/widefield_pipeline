    Summary · status of each result

    Where things stand

    Post-stroke CHANGE claims are judged in the frozen pre-stroke encoder (one model per animal from its August pre sessions, applied unchanged), with FaceRhythm inside it; per-session refit results are supporting only. Figure links jump to the supporting figures.

    Bottom line (updated 10-28, after the overnight controls; the 10-25 version is in FINDINGS.md section 0): Behaviour mostly recovers — on the first-lick aim axis 25% of the acute far-contra deficit remains chronically (PS94 45%) (Key figure 1A). The cortical position code is not lost, it is read differently: decoders refit after the stroke read lick position at 90–100% of pre while the frozen pre-stroke read-out stays at ~75% for ITI licks and ~70% for the far-contra spout code on hits (10.2b, 1B–C). Self-initiated (ITI) licks are read ~0.25 mm contralesional of where they land, against pre licks that landed at the same place and had the same previous-trial target, 4/4 in every epoch; covariates, a non-linear decoder and a movement-only decoder rule out the movement as the cause (1D, 12.15, 11.15). The cued “read ahead of the failing movement” offset is mostly target composition: against pre licks of the same target that landed at the same place it is +0.01 (n.s., 2/4) — post-stroke misses are read toward their target exactly as pre-stroke misses were (12.15); within a cued bout the tongue corrects toward the target lick by lick while the read-out stays put (Key figure 2). Maps: with FWER max-statistic tests the ITI lateral decoder pattern does not change shape, while the cued lateral pattern changes acutely and chronically (15.17); in pixel mixed models the cued lateral map drops, partly returns and stays changed even where aim has recovered, while far-contra maps scale with the aim deficit (15.21, 15.22); the large cortex-wide components of the unscaled ITI maps are mostly a variance effect. Encoder (frozen, 10-25, unchanged): acutely cortical activity stays mostly consistent with the worse movement under the pre-stroke mapping plus a transient orofacial-specific disruption (6.2); the movement → cortex mapping is partly remapped chronically (2.3, 4.1), and the large chronic mapping change (Layer 2, 19.1) is cortex-wide and grows with time; cortex and behaviour recover with no consistent order (0.5). Full reasoning: FINDINGS.md section 0 and 4d0-NIGHT.

    Direction is open: encoding and decoding measure shared variance between cortex and movement; “cortex follows the movement” and “cortex drives the movement” both fit them. Tests planned: within-trial lead / lag (does pre-lick cortex predict the lick’s direction?), lagged FaceRhythm kernels, pre-cue → lick coupling, hits-only recovery timing.

    What persists chronically, and where it is shown:

    • A cue-window orofacial residual (cortex responds more than the pre-stroke mapping predicts, beyond control cortex): orofacial − control +0.88 pre-SD (3/4; +1.33, 4/4 without FaceRhythm); lateral frontal / orofacial areas; still above pre at full behavioural recovery (+0.73, z 3.2).     • Less spout-specific tuning: target-unique coding stays down (0/4 up; Figs 0.3, 0.9); the PETH spout code does not recover (cortex lick −0.096, 0/4; Figs 0.4, 0.10); face-direction coupling with SSp-m falls (Figs 0.2, 22.1); the pre-stroke spatial template’s shape stays off 4/4 (imaging page, FINDINGS 3.12).
