    Where things stand

    Post-stroke CHANGE claims are judged in the frozen pre-stroke encoder (one model per animal from its August pre sessions, applied unchanged), with FaceRhythm inside it; per-session refit results are supporting only. Figure links jump to the supporting figures.

    Bottom line (updated 10-28, after the overnight controls; the 10-25 version is in FINDINGS.md section 0): Behaviour mostly recovers — on the first-lick aim axis 25% of the acute far-contra deficit remains chronically (PS94 45%) (Key figure 1A). The cortical position code is not lost, it is read differently: decoders refit after the stroke read lick position at 90–100% of pre while the frozen pre-stroke read-out stays at ~75% for ITI licks and ~70% for the far-contra spout code on hits (10.2b, 1B–C). Self-initiated (ITI) licks are read ~0.25 mm contralesional of where they land, against pre licks that landed at the same place and had the same previous-trial target, 4/4 in every epoch; covariates, a non-linear decoder and a movement-only decoder rule out the movement as the cause (1D, 12.15, 11.21). The cued “read ahead of the failing movement” offset is mostly target composition: against pre licks of the same target that landed at the same place it is +0.01 (n.s., 2/4) — post-stroke misses are read toward their target exactly as pre-stroke misses were (12.15); within a cued bout the tongue corrects toward the target lick by lick while the read-out stays put (Key figure 2). Maps: with FWER max-statistic tests the ITI lateral decoder pattern does not change shape, while the cued lateral pattern changes acutely and chronically (15.17); in pixel mixed models the cued lateral map drops, partly returns and stays changed even where aim has recovered, while far-contra maps scale with the aim deficit (15.21, 15.22); the large cortex-wide components of the unscaled ITI maps are mostly a variance effect. Encoder (frozen, 10-25, unchanged): acutely cortical activity stays mostly consistent with the worse movement under the pre-stroke mapping plus a transient orofacial-specific disruption (6.2); the movement → cortex mapping is partly remapped chronically (2.3, 4.1), and the large chronic mapping change (Layer 2, 19.1) is cortex-wide and grows with time; cortex and behaviour recover with no consistent order (0.5).     What the balance is — and is not (10-28 late). (a) A REAL hemispheric shift, not an artefact of how it is computed: it survives the extrapolation control for cued licks (0.53 vs days 0.23), a lateral-position reference, lick-count weighting and equal area weights, and it tracks the decoder’s offset session by session (cued r −0.58 to −0.92, 4/4). (b) A STATE, not a forecast: no prediction of the next session’s change; within a known animal the calendar already tracks recovery. (c) AFTER the movement, not before: trial by trial, the balance 0.1–0.5 s after the first lick follows that lick’s direction (r 0.3–0.5) — but equally BEFORE the stroke (the normal hemispheric direction code) — and the balance in the 0.5 s before the lick predicts nothing about it (r ≈ 0, every epoch). So no evidence that a pre-movement imbalance causes the error; the stroke SHIFTS the post-lick code across sessions. (e) The balance is only PART of that shift: it explains ~35–50% of the ITI decoder offset (12.18), decoders blind to the hemispheric difference keep the full offset (12.21), and for ITI licks the decoder offset itself is the better recovery marker (unseen animal 0.39, central landings 0.35, vs balance 0.07 without extrapolation).
