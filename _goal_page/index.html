    Biology in brief

    Click a card to jump to its figure. Hypothesis (revised 10-29 with Priya): after the ipsilesional striatal lesion, orofacial cortex shows three things. (1) Cued licks: the acute hemispheric imbalance and the contralesional decoder offset are largely REDUNDANT with the landing error (cortex carries the target spout; the tongue lands ipsilesionally) and appear after the lick; whether they drive the deficit or recovery is untested. Before the cue the target code is weaker acutely but not re-aimed, and errors look like hits until after the cue — as they do pre-stroke. (2) A persistent contralesional state shift on spontaneous ITI licks: read as more contralesional than pre-stroke licks landing at the same place (+0.29 / +0.19 / +0.22 mm acute / subacute / chronic, 4/4), appearing at lick onset, strongest in SSp-n, and not gone chronically; it HOLDS on licks made while the spout is docked (stationary), whereas the ITI hemispheric balance and its link to recovery come from licks made while the spout is moving (retraction / outbound; see the docked-spout supplement, 14.18). (3) Slow reorganisation: for the same target and landing place, the contralesional hemisphere’s share of the cued lick response rises above pre over weeks (+0.13 / +0.16, 4/4). The rise is not confined to orofacial cortex (visual and retrosplenial areas rise too), so it may be a cortex-wide shift in hemispheric balance rather than orofacial-specific reorganisation. Whether it supports recovery is testable by chronic contralesional silencing. Open: motor command vs sensory reafference; causal roles. All PROVISIONAL.

    Behaviour
    After the stroke the first lick to the far-contralesional spout lands ipsilesionally; aim recovers over ~2 weeks.
    Lick-evoked activity
    Orofacial cortex responds MORE to licks after the stroke, in both hemispheres; the size of that change does not track recovery (its course differs with lesion size / history).
    The lick code shifts
    A frozen pre-stroke decoder reads licks as more contralesional than where they land (like-for-like offset) — persistent for self-initiated licks.
    Where the shift lives
    Split exactly by area, most of that offset is a left/right difference; a broad sensorimotor left/right imbalance tracks recovery within every animal, strongest in SSp-n (nose / upper-lip S1).
    The hemispheric balance
    Each hemisphere’s response relative to what pre-stroke licks in the same direction gave: acutely the ipsilesional hemisphere over-responds; it re-balances with recovery.
    A recovery marker
    The balance after the first post-cue lick places an UNSEEN animal on its recovery curve ~3× better than the calendar — a state marker, not a forecast. Caveat: for cued licks it largely re-describes the landing error (cortex partly codes the target); the ITI shift survives that.
    Before the cue
    Waiting at the hardest (far-contralesional) target, contralesional orofacial cortex sits at a sustained raised level acutely.
    Sign conventions (read before comparing figures; 10-29). Two kinds of quantity are both called “contralesional”:
    • DIRECTION quantities, + = toward the contralesional side: lick angle; aim (+ = more contralesional, − = ipsilesional = worse for far-contra); decoder like-for-like offset (+ = the lick is READ as more contralesional than it landed); SHIFT in the shift / gain / track decompositions (13.8).
    • HEMISPHERE quantities, + = contralesional HEMISPHERE larger: the hemispheric balance log2(contra obs/pred) − log2(ipsi obs/pred) as stored and in older figures; per-area left/right differences dhr−dhl (12.17 checks).
    In the normal pre-stroke code each hemisphere responds more to licks toward the OPPOSITE side, so a LOWER hemispheric balance looks like a MORE contralesional lick. The key balance figures (12.19, 14.14, 14.17, 14.18) are therefore shown as IMPLIED DIRECTION = −(hemispheric balance): + = the activity looks like a more contralesional lick than the one made — the same direction as the decoder offset. Acute values are then positive. Older figures keep the hemispheric sign; their captions say so. Correlation signs flip with the display (a balance that rises with better aim is an implied direction that falls with it); cross-validated R² does not. Image side: ipsilesional = image LEFT on brain maps.

    Where things stand

    Post-stroke CHANGE claims are judged in the frozen pre-stroke encoder (one model per animal from its August pre sessions, applied unchanged), with FaceRhythm inside it; per-session refit results are supporting only. Figure links jump to the supporting figures.

    Bottom line (updated 10-28, after the overnight controls; the 10-25 version is in FINDINGS.md section 0): Behaviour mostly recovers — on the first-lick aim axis 25% of the acute far-contra deficit remains chronically (PS94 45%) (Key figure 1A). The cortical position code is not lost, it is read differently: decoders refit after the stroke read lick position at 90–100% of pre while the frozen pre-stroke read-out stays at ~75% for ITI licks and ~70% for the far-contra spout code on hits (10.2b, 1B–C). Self-initiated (ITI) licks are read ~0.25 mm contralesional of where they land, against pre licks that landed at the same place and had the same previous-trial target, 4/4 in every epoch; covariates, a non-linear decoder and a movement-only decoder rule out the movement as the cause (1D, 12.15, 11.21). The cued “read ahead of the failing movement” offset is mostly target composition: against pre licks of the same target that landed at the same place it is +0.01 (n.s., 2/4) — post-stroke misses are read toward their target exactly as pre-stroke misses were (12.15); within a cued bout the tongue corrects toward the target lick by lick while the read-out stays put (Key figure 2). Maps: with FWER max-statistic tests the ITI lateral decoder pattern does not change shape, while the cued lateral pattern changes acutely and chronically (15.17); in pixel mixed models the cued lateral map drops, partly returns and stays changed even where aim has recovered, while far-contra maps scale with the aim deficit (15.21, 15.22); the large cortex-wide components of the unscaled ITI maps are mostly a variance effect. Encoder (frozen, 10-25, unchanged): acutely cortical activity stays mostly consistent with the worse movement under the pre-stroke mapping plus a transient orofacial-specific disruption (6.2); the movement → cortex mapping is partly remapped chronically (2.3, 4.1), and the large chronic mapping change (Layer 2, 19.1) is cortex-wide and grows with time; cortex and behaviour recover with no consistent order (0.5).     Direction is open: encoding and decoding measure shared variance between cortex and movement; “cortex follows the movement” and “cortex drives the movement” both fit them.     What the balance is — and is not (10-28 late). (a) A REAL hemispheric shift, not an artefact of how it is computed: it survives the extrapolation control for cued licks (0.53 vs days 0.23), a lateral-position reference, lick-count weighting and equal area weights, and it tracks the decoder’s offset session by session (cued r −0.58 to −0.92, 4/4). (b) A STATE, not a forecast: no prediction of the next session’s change; within a known animal the calendar already tracks recovery. (c) AFTER the movement, not before: trial by trial, the balance 0.1–0.5 s after the first lick follows that lick’s direction (r 0.3–0.5) — but equally BEFORE the stroke (the normal hemispheric direction code) — and the balance in the 0.5 s before the lick predicts nothing about it (r ≈ 0, every epoch). So no evidence that a pre-movement imbalance causes the error; the stroke SHIFTS the post-lick code across sessions. (e) The balance is only PART of that shift: it explains ~35–50% of the ITI decoder offset (12.18), decoders blind to the hemispheric difference keep the full offset (12.21), and for ITI licks the decoder offset itself is the better recovery marker (unseen animal 0.39, central landings 0.35, vs balance 0.07 without extrapolation). So the post-stroke direction change lives in the PATTERN across orofacial areas; the left / right balance is its simplest summary, which generalises best across animals for cued licks.
