## 0f. 2026-10-30 — why T·415 does not match the 470 lick dip; native maps; PS120

**The correction arithmetic is right** (`cohort2_firstlick_channels_daq.png`, rows adopted | masked: corrected =
470 − T·415). **The single coefficient is what is off.** At the post-lick dip (0.8-2 s), 470/415 is 2.1-2.5 in
PS120-122, but T·415/415 is ~1.4-1.5 (masked). So the corrected trace keeps a −0.14 to −0.30% dip. In PS118 470
barely dips (ratio 0.16), so subtracting the 415 dip ADDS a +0.9% bump at 1 s and a −0.8% undershoot at 2.5-4 s.
Three causes, measured on the cortex mean:
1. **The 415 frame is acquired 16 ms (one camera frame) BEFORE its 470 partner, and the correction treats them as
   simultaneous.** The 415→470 transfer phase is exactly a 16 ms delay: +10-12° at 1-3 Hz, +27-30° at 3-7 Hz,
   +55-61° at the heartbeat (360·f·0.016 s). Interpolating 415 to the 470 frame times takes the phase to ~0° in
   every session. It raises the 0.1-14 Hz scalar slope only 3-7% (PS121 1.38 → 1.48), but the heartbeat-band
   correction is badly under-fitted without it (cos 58° ≈ 0.53).
2. **The hemodynamic gain depends on frequency, and the scalar fit sits below it.** Transfer gain (real part, 415
   as acquired): PS121 0.1-0.3 Hz 2.4, 0.3-1 Hz 2.6, 1-3 Hz 2.1, 3-7 Hz 1.7. PS122 2.3 / 2.4 / 2.0 / 1.7. PS120
   2.1 / 2.0 / 1.8 / 1.6. PS118 1.6 / 1.6 / 1.4 / 1.2. The per-pixel rcoeff median is 1.4-1.5 (masked). That is
   diluted by 415 variance not shared with 470 (coherence 0.7-0.9) and by the lag.
3. In PS118 a large sustained calcium response fills the 470 dip, so the dip cannot be used as a hemodynamic
   reference there. The scalar fit is also confounded with neural signal in any task-locked window.
**Next variant to test (not adopted):** 415 interpolated to the 470 time (lag fix), plus a coefficient fitted
where hemodynamics dominate (e.g. 1-8 Hz, or quiet/ITI periods), compared on residual 415 coherence, the residual
lick dip, and decoding. One coefficient per pixel cannot be right at every frequency; a short FIR (or band-wise)
415→470 kernel is the general form.

**BRAIN MASK WAS CUTTING OUT ANTERIOR CORTEX (fixed 10-30).** Every QC script used `F470 > 0.45 * max`, eroded 4 px. The anterior cortex is dimmer and mottled in the 470 mean image, so it was fragmented away. Anterior-third coverage was 4% (PS121) / 6% (PS122) / 12-14% (Thy1); with `scripts/cohort2_qc/brainmask.py` (dark-relative 20% cut, largest component, holes filled) it is 33-44% (`cohort2_brainmask_old_vs_new.png`). Native maps are re-rendered with it, and frontal cortex (ALM / tjM1 territory) responds strongly in PS118 / PS121 / PS122. **All cortex-MEAN numbers in §0-0f used the old mask, so they are posterior-weighted; rerun before quoting.** The 470/415 mean-image ratio also varies 2-3x across the field (bright lateral/posterior), so the two LEDs illuminate the field differently (rig check).

**Native maps** (no Allen; `native_maps_1029.py`, `labcams/cohort2/native_maps_20261029/`): per session, the
cue response (0-1 s) by spout position, all trials, first lick (0-0.4 s) and L−R, swap-masked correction, DAQ
events. In `cohort2_1029_native_summary.png`, PS118 and PS121 show focal bilateral hotspots. PS122 is broad, with
a visible VESSEL pattern (residual hemodynamics, consistent with item 2). PS120 is ±0.1-0.2% with an edge artifact:
**no clear calcium response**. Its raw 470 lick response is +0.08% then a −0.6% cortex-wide hemodynamic dip
(PS118 +3.1%). So PS120 is not inverted: its GCaMP signal is absent or very weak (expression / window / focus;
F 16.8k vs 25k; per-pixel signal at the shot floor, §4).

**Deck**: `labcams/cohort2/cross-session_preprocessing.pptx` (motion QC + photobleach per mouse). The

## 0e. 2026-10-30 — first-lick response with DAQ timing; does the SVD need redoing?

**The "negative first-lick response" was a clock error, not signal.** iso_check.py / masked_vs_adopted_1029.py took
event times from the behaviour PC log, which carries a constant offset of ~1 s. With lick (`lick_analog`) and cue
from the DAQ, mapped through the pipeline's own `_frames`, the corrected cortex-mean first-lick response is
POSITIVE in every mouse, peaking 0.0-0.3 s after the lick:

| | adopted 0-0.5 s | masked 0-0.5 s | masked peak (time) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS118 | +1.85% | +1.84% | +2.21% (0.19 s) |
| PS120 | +0.03% | +0.02% | (flat; photon-limited, §4) |
| PS121 | +0.54% | +0.38% | +0.54% (0.10 s) |
| PS122 | +1.00% | +0.63% | +0.92% (0.13 s) |

**Why masking shrinks the RiboL1 response, and why that is right.** Raw channels lick-locked (DAQ timing, swap frames
out; figure `cohort2_firstlick_channels_daq.png`): in EVERY mouse, Thy1 included, 415 has a fast positive transient
at the lick, the same shape as 470's peak, then a dip at ~1-1.4 s. The 470:415 peak ratio is ~2.5-3 in both genotypes
(PS118 3.7 vs 1.3%, PS121 0.85 vs 0.3%, PS122 1.75 vs 0.6%). PS120 shows a sharp spike in both channels at t = 0. So
the fast 415 transient is lick-locked and shared by both indicators: most likely lick/jaw movement artifact plus the
early hemodynamic change, NOT GCaMP8s-specific calcium in 415. Subtracting it is the purpose of the correction. The
adopted RiboL1 product barely subtracted it (rcoeff ~0.1-0.4), which inflated the response. The ~1.3 s dip is the
usual delayed hemodynamic undershoot.

**SVD: probably not required, but cleanest for PS121/PS122.** wfield `approximate_svd` builds U from 1000 bins of 15
frames (~6.6% of the session), then projects EVERY frame. So a swapped frame affects (i) its own SVT column, which the
mask interpolates away, and (ii) U only if it fell in a sampled bin (expected ~4 of PS121's 60), diluted 1/15.
Per-component check: the share of each component's energy in the bad frames is up to 51% (PS121 k10). In RiboL1, 76
(PS121) / 30 (PS122) of 100 components have >10%, against 1 (PS118) / 4 (PS120) in Thy1. But a +100% frame projects
onto every component, so this measures how big the bad frames are, not how much U was bent. No component is
swap-only (all are ≥49% ordinary signal). To be sure for RiboL1, re-run the SVD on the imaging box with the flagged
frames replaced by their same-LED neighbours before binning/projection (small wrapper around the dat memmap), then
compare the corrected products. Not needed for Thy1 or cohort1.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS118 Thy1 | 1.18 → 1.19 | .009 → .007 | 3% → 3% | +0.13 → +0.14% |
| PS120 Thy1 | 1.33 → 1.49 | .089 → .000 | 43% → 45% | +0.11 → +0.08% |
| PS121 RiboL1 | **0.11 → 1.50** | **.41 → .004** | 56% → 55% | **−0.78 → −0.47%** |

* **Masking fixes the RiboL1 hemodynamic correction.** The coefficient comes out like Thy1's, and the corrected signal
  no longer carries 415's fast (0.1-14 Hz) hemodynamics. The RiboL1 post-lick cortex-wide dip shrinks by 40% (PS121),

  residual hemodynamics; the R² does.
