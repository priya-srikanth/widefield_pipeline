**Kinetics adjustment.** Each 8s recording is filtered with the exact ratio of the two indicator kernels (unit-area
difference of exponentials). This gives what a 6s would have reported for the same activity. The 6s sessions are also
sharpened to 8s as a check, and PS120 goes through both. Kernel constants are literature ASSUMPTIONS, not fitted:
6s rise tau 0.10 s / decay t1/2 1.0 s, 8s 0.02 s / 0.30 s, and a 3 x 3 grid of decay half-times (6s 0.6-1.5 s,
8s 0.2-0.45 s). **Check that the kernels are sensible:** converted 8s first-lick waveforms peak at 0.29-0.61 s and
fall to half in 0.54-0.80 s (cortex), inside the measured 6s range (0.22-0.45 s, 0.48-1.15 s).

* **The amplitude gap is not kinetics.** With the kinetics matched, RiboL1-8s responses are 10-20x smaller than 6s,
  and d' stays lower in both directions of the conversion. Every grid cell agrees (hotspot 0.15-1.1%, d' 0.96-2.2).
* **8s maps are MORE spread out, not more local.** Their maps are flatter: brain mean / hotspot 0.47-0.55 vs 0.31-0.46,
  and half of the brain or more above half the hotspot. Matching the kinetics makes this slightly stronger, and every
  grid cell agrees (0.49-0.57). The 6s maps have bilateral lateral (orofacial / ALM-ward) hotspots with a clear
  posterior fall-off. The 8s maps also respond medially and posteriorly, with visible vessels and the midline sinus.
* **Part of that spread looks vascular.** The mean-removed 8s maps have finer structure (31-41 px vs 45-62), in the
  null's range (26-41). The null's 10-30 map shows the same positive midline sinus. So the finer 8s structure cannot
  be credited to soma-targeted calcium yet. It is equally consistent with the residual vessel pattern standing out
  against a 3-4x smaller signal. Whole-map spread is NOT from the null pattern (the null's own map is local: 0.06-0.31).
* **No spout-specificity advantage.** The absolute spout-specific signal in 8s is 0.11-0.15% (0.03-0.05% matched),
  only ~2x the null's 0.06-0.07%. Relative to the hotspot it is 6s-like for PS122 (0.07-0.13) and higher for PS121
  (0.18-0.19). But a ratio rises when the shared response is small: the null scores 0.25-0.40. So PS121's higher
  share cannot be told apart from non-calcium, spout-dependent signal.
* **Ongoing activity.** With the global signal removed, short-range correlation is slightly LOWER in 8s (0.73-0.78 vs
  0.80-0.84 at 25 px), between 6s and the null. That is the direction expected from more noise or vessel signal, not
  from sharper neural fields. With the global signal kept, all groups overlap.
* **Bottom line.** On these data, RiboL1-8s gives a much smaller signal and lower single-trial detectability than
  Thy1-6s, even with the kinetics matched. It also gives no measurable gain in regional or spout specificity. Its one
  real advantage is speed. Caveats: n = 1 6s mouse in cohort2 (plus 4 cohort1 references from 2.5 months earlier),
  2 RiboL1 mice, 2 days. Kernels are assumed; px not converted to mm (no cohort2 atlas registration yet).

**CORRECTION (Priya 10-31): for jGCaMP8s the 415 signal also RISES with calcium below its isosbestic point (from
the spectra).** So the 8s 415 carries same-sign calcium, the correction subtracts real signal, and the "inverted
calcium" reasoning in the next paragraph does not apply. Its sign test is still a valid measurement, but in-phase is
what both calcium and hemodynamics now predict, so it cannot separate them.

**Vessel vs parenchyma split** (`iso_vessel_split.py`, first-lick raw 415 / raw 470; vessels = >= 10% darker than
the local mean of the 470 image, 13-17% of brain; parenchymal hotspot = top 2% of the corrected map, off vessels).
Calcium in 415 predicts a hotspot 415/470 ratio in 8s above 6s's and above the vessel ratio.

| | vessel 415/470 | parenchyma | parenchymal hotspot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6s PS118, PS92, PS93, PS95 | 0.28-0.30 | 0.21-0.26 | **0.08-0.15** |
| 6s PS94_0811 | 0.49 | 0.54 | 0.36 |
| PS121 8s | 0.26-0.39 | 0.20-0.31 | **0.04-0.15** |

* **PS121: no sign of calcium in 415.** Its hotspot ratio is 6s-like, and its 415 lick response is strongest on vessels.
* **PS122: ambiguous.** Its 415 is about 1/3 of 470 everywhere, hotspot and vessels alike (parenchymal map r .95). That
  is more than 6s at the hotspot. But the same flat pattern is seen in a 6s mouse (PS94_0811), and PS121 (same
  indicator) does not show it. Spectral calcium in 415 is a property of the indicator, so it should appear in both.
* **Calcium in 415 only SCALES the corrected signal.** With 415 = k*Ca + h and 470 = Ca + g*h, the corrected
  `470 - g*415` = (1 - g*k) Ca. Maps, spread, spout share and kinetics keep their shape; only the amplitude (and d')
  shrink. From the kept fractions (g ~ 1.3-1.5): PS122 implies k ~ 0.4, PS121 k ~ 0.05-0.2. One indicator should give
  one k. Given k from the spectra, both mice can be rescaled by 1/(1 - g*k).
* **Upper bound:** even if all of PS122's removed signal is calcium, its raw 470 lick response (2.0-3.3% at the
  hotspot) is still 2-3x below 6s (5.0-6.8%). PS121 is 6x below either way.

**If the jGCaMP8s isosbestic point is ~440 nm (Priya 10-31), is 415 carrying INVERTED calcium?** (superseded by the
correction above) Below the isosbestic
point, calcium makes 415 go down. Then (a) `470 - b*415` ADDS calcium, so the corrected 8s signal would be an
overestimate, never the cause of a small one; (b) the fitted b is biased low, so some blood flow is left behind.
Sign test (`iso_phase.py`, raw 415 vs raw 470, signed coherence = real part of the band coherency, whole session):

* **No anti-phase anywhere.** At the cortex mean and at the lick hotspot, in every band (0.1-13 Hz), all 12 sessions
  are in phase: signed coherence +0.4 to +0.98. The phase falls linearly with frequency in every group (+10-30°
  at 0.3 Hz to about -55° at 10 Hz), i.e. the same small fixed 415-470 time offset, not a sign flip.
* RiboL1 (8s) looks like the null, not like an inverted reference. Hotspot 2-5 Hz: PS122 +0.92-0.93, PS120 +0.91-0.94,
  6s +0.40-0.84, PS121 +0.63-0.80. Only PS121 10-29 at 5-8 Hz dips (+0.40), still positive.
* So any inverted calcium in the 8s 415 is small next to its in-phase hemodynamics. It cannot hide signal, because
  the correction would add it rather than remove it.
* A ~440 nm reference would not be a drop-in fix. Hemoglobin absorption is very different at 440 (near the deoxy-Hb
  peak) from 415, so it would track oxygenation as well as blood volume.

## 0m. 2026-10-30 — pre-SVD repair (now DEFAULT), saturation, per-pixel coefficient, the 10-09 pilot

**Pre-SVD other-LED frame repair: `wfield_local/swap_repair.py`, ON by default in `run_wfield_local`**
(`--no-swap-repair` to disable). One streaming pass over the motion-corrected movie (8x8 block means). Per pair, slot
and 16 row bands it fits d ~ alpha + beta * P, where d = frame / own-slot neighbour median - 1 and P = other-slot / own
- 1:
* a band is bad if beta > 0.08 and > 8 robust SD above that band's median;
* a WEAK candidate (strongest band beta < 0.5) is kept only if isolated in time (both neighbours < 0.4x its beta),
  because a swap is one exposure;
* both slots fully swapped → exchanged; anything else (single swap, rolling-shutter band, dual exposure) →
  replaced by its nearest good same-slot neighbours;
* `frames_average` is corrected exactly; the raw mean is kept as `frames_average_raw.npy`. Writes `swap_repair.npz` /
  `.json`, and the summary goes into `local_wfield_summary.json`.
* The view (`RepairedDat`) has no `filename`, so wfield `approximate_svd` reads it through slices in BOTH its
  binning and projection passes.
* Tests (`tests/test_swap_repair.py`, synthetic RiboL1-like and equal-brightness movies): finds a full swap, an
  exchanged pair, a band and a dual exposure, with no false positives; restores frames; `approximate_svd` runs on it.
* On real data (SVD-reconstructed windows around known bad frames, 4 sessions): it catches every brightness-flagged
  (unambiguous) swap with 0 extra detections. It also showed that most PS122 "pattern-only" flags from `swap_scan`
  were weak TWO-frame events with a global rise, i.e. physiology, not swaps. So the 10-29 PS122 swap mask (0.35%)
  over-excluded some real frames (harmless, but PS122's swap count was overstated).
* **Re-run 10-29 on the imaging box** (keeps motion correction; redoes SVD + repair + hemo variant):
  `python -m wfield_local.preprocess 20261029 --only PS118 PS120 PS121 PS122 --redo-svd --skip-maps --skip-xall --skip-crossday-intensity`
  New `preprocess --redo-svd` flag. Afterwards the M: `swapped_pairs.npz` and `hemo_*_swapmask` dirs for 10-29 describe

## 0f. 2026-10-30 — why T·415 does not match the 470 lick dip; native maps; PS120

**The correction arithmetic is right** (`cohort2_firstlick_channels_daq.png`, rows adopted | masked: corrected =
470 − T·415). **The single coefficient is what is off.** At the post-lick dip (0.8-2 s), 470/415 is 2.1-2.5 in
PS120-122, but T·415/415 is ~1.4-1.5 (masked). So the corrected trace keeps a −0.14 to −0.30% dip. In PS118 470
barely dips (ratio 0.16), so subtracting the 415 dip ADDS a +0.9% bump at 1 s and a −0.8% undershoot at 2.5-4 s.
Three causes, measured on the cortex mean:
1. **The 415 frame is acquired 16 ms (one camera frame) BEFORE its 470 partner, and the correction treats them as
   simultaneous.** The 415→470 transfer phase is exactly a 16 ms delay: +10-12° at 1-3 Hz, +27-30° at 3-7 Hz,
   +55-61° at the heartbeat (360·f·0.016 s). Interpolating 415 to the 470 frame times takes the phase to ~0° in
   every session. It raises the 0.1-14 Hz scalar slope only 3-7% (PS121 1.38 → 1.48), but the heartbeat-band
   correction is badly under-fitted without it (cos 58° ≈ 0.53).
2. **The hemodynamic gain depends on frequency, and the scalar fit sits below it.** Transfer gain (real part, 415
   as acquired): PS121 0.1-0.3 Hz 2.4, 0.3-1 Hz 2.6, 1-3 Hz 2.1, 3-7 Hz 1.7. PS122 2.3 / 2.4 / 2.0 / 1.7. PS120
   2.1 / 2.0 / 1.8 / 1.6. PS118 1.6 / 1.6 / 1.4 / 1.2. The per-pixel rcoeff median is 1.4-1.5 (masked). That is
   diluted by 415 variance not shared with 470 (coherence 0.7-0.9) and by the lag.
3. In PS118 a large sustained calcium response fills the 470 dip, so the dip cannot be used as a hemodynamic
   reference there. The scalar fit is also confounded with neural signal in any task-locked window.
**Next variant to test (not adopted):** 415 interpolated to the 470 time (lag fix), plus a coefficient fitted
where hemodynamics dominate (e.g. 1-8 Hz, or quiet/ITI periods), compared on residual 415 coherence, the residual
lick dip, and decoding. One coefficient per pixel cannot be right at every frequency; a short FIR (or band-wise)
415→470 kernel is the general form.

`fit_vs_output_demo.py` demonstrates it: the 0.1 Hz zero-phase high-pass used for the **coefficient fit**
is acausal — it deposits **−0.209 in the 1 s before an impulse** (measured −0.2086, matches
`PREPROCESSING_DECISION.md`). That backward shadow is harmless for estimating a single 415→470 slope but
would smear every cue response backward if used on the **output**; so the output goes through the meegkit
polynomial detrend, which preserves event timing. Fit band-selects hemodynamics; output preserves time.
