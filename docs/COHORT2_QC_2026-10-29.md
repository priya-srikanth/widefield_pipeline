## 2. The 415 "dimmed a TON" question → usable as is; do NOT raise the 415 LED
Full table/reasoning in `docs/NEW_COHORT_2026-10-29.md`. Summary: the RiboL1 415 is the *brightest* of the
cohort (~30,600 counts over offset, 22–24× the camera floor, shot-noise ΔF/F floor ~0.84%). The "dimming"
is a **within-session fade (bleaching)**, not a low level; PS121 415 −34%, PS122 470 −41%/415 flat. It is a
software (detrend) matter, not an LED one — raising 415 would worsen the fade for no SNR gain.

## 3. Slow drift that survives `meegkit_hpfit` — "43 min is too slow" (correct)
The adopted detrend is an order-10 robust polynomial with a **~43 min 50% cutoff**, tuned to leave the
57–121 s position-block band untouched (retention 0.99 @300 s, 0.98 @600 s). By design it removes almost
nothing in the 3–15 min range where cohort2's bleaching drift actually lives.

Corrected cortex-mean, slow (≥period) share of variance (`slow_spectrum.py`):

| session | >1800 s | >900 s | >600 s | >300 s | >121 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS118 Thy1 (bright) | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 9 |
| PS120 Thy1 (dim) | 1 | 9 | 24 | 45 | 68 |
| PS121 RiboL1 | 2 | 14 | 28 | 45 | 53 |

The 43 min cutoff catches only the >1800 s tail (1–2%). The drift sits at ~3–15 min and **butts against the
57–121 s block band and the ~180–300 s engagement/activity scale**, so a faster cutoff cannot cleanly
separate drift from real slow signal. Cohort1 already measured the consequence: `meegkit_2band` (removing
the ≥300 s shared 415/470 band from the product) **lowered** pre-cue decoding 26/32 sessions (−0.016,
p=4e-4; `scripts/slowband_decode_check.py`). The surviving slow band also does **not** track the raw bleach
trajectory (|r|≤0.22 for the two bad sessions; `slow_is_bleach.py`) — i.e. it is not cleanly removable
"bleach residual".

**Recommendation:** keep `meegkit_hpfit` as the shared product (don't switch methods, don't adopt
`meegkit_2band`). Remove slow drift **analysis-side only**, inside continuous-time models, via the existing
600 s Frisch-Waugh-Lovell projection (`combined_encoding.py` `DRIFT_MIN_PERIOD_S`), and only after a decode
check shows removal helps on cohort2's own data (needs Allen ROIs → waits on cross-registration). Trimming
the head is **not** the fix: there is no fast settling transient (`settle_s = 0`, `early_fade.py`).

## 4. PS120 is photon-limited, not a state problem (behaviour was good)
Per-pixel corrected ΔF/F amplitude vs single-frame shot-noise floor (`perpixel_snr.py`, `iso_snr.py`):

| session | F0 | per-pixel ΔF/F SD | shot floor | signal/floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS118 Thy1 (bright) | 26,920 | 3.37% | 0.95% | 3.5× |
| PS120 Thy1 (dim) | 16,067 | 1.18% | 1.21% | ~1.0× |
| PS121 RiboL1 | 22,229 | 1.41% | 0.84% | 1.7× |

PS120's per-pixel signal sits **at its shot-noise floor** (after SVD denoising) with the lowest baseline F →
**light starvation** (window clarity / focus / z-drift / expression), not animal state. Next time PS120 is
imaged, check focus/window first.

## 5. GCaMP8s SNR and the pilot's "fine regionalisation"
PS121 (8s) per-pixel ΔF/F is 1.41%, **1.7× above its shot floor** — real signal, consistent with the raw
"% of start" spread. Lower amplitude than bright GCaMP6s (expected: faster 8s decay → less per-session
low-frequency power). **Per-pixel ≈ cortex-mean (ratio 1.1) for all three** → in the **awake, task-engaged**
state the corrected signal is spatially fairly coherent; the fine, high-rank structure the 10-09 pilot saw
was largely the **anesthetised** state, not the indicator. So the GCaMP6s-vs-8s regional/kinetics question
is now answerable **state-matched** on this awake task data (exploratory, task #7), with the pilot confound
removed.

**Kinetics caveat:** GCaMP8s decay τ ~ hundreds of ms and rise ~tens of ms. The 2 s bins used in the slow
-drift figures are display-only; any kinetics/evoked analysis for the RiboL1 animals must use frame
resolution (~32 ms) — and even then the 8s *rise* is near the sampling limit (report as a bound).

## 6. Fit vs output in `meegkit_hpfit` (why two drift removers)
`fit_vs_output_demo.py` demonstrates it: the 0.1 Hz zero-phase high-pass used for the **coefficient fit**
is acausal — it deposits **−0.209 in the 1 s before an impulse** (measured −0.2086, matches
`PREPROCESSING_DECISION.md`). That backward shadow is harmless for estimating a single 415→470 slope but
would smear every cue response backward if used on the **output**; so the output goes through the meegkit
polynomial detrend, which preserves event timing. Fit band-selects hemodynamics; output preserves time.
