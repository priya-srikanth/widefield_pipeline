# Cue + 70 ms contact floor: is 70 ms right? (2026-11-04)

Question (Priya 11-04): spout contacts count as a cue response only from cue + 70 ms
(`trial_classes.CONTACT_FROM_CUE_S`, `pose_kinematics_demo.daq_contacts_ms`). Earlier licks go to `licks_precue.csv`
(strobe .. cue + 70 ms). Is 70 ms too long? If we keep it, should licks in 0-70 ms be excluded from pre-cue as well?

**Answer: keep 70 ms.** Contacts in 0-70 ms are not cue responses. They also do not continue a bout that runs through
the cue. They are isolated spontaneous or anticipatory contacts at a flat background rate. The cue-driven contact rise
starts at 80-90 ms. Moving the floor to 0 or 50 ms would change the class of 3 or 2 trials in the whole cohort. A
100 ms floor would cut real responses (PS94/PS95). Excluding 0-70 ms from pre-cue as well (Priya's proposal) is
supported by the data. It removes 2277 DAQ contacts (4.0 % of trials) and 608 camera licks from `licks_precue.csv`.

Data: 107 curated cohort-1 sessions (August pre + all post; PS93_0813 has no v36 outputs), 56,979 trials. Source:
DAQ contact onsets `dlc_frames.lick_onsets` (= `daq_trials.decode` lick_s, the same source as `daq_contacts_ms`).
Cue = v36 `windows.npz` cue_daq (as in `trial_classes`), which agrees with the behavior table's DAQ cue to <= 14 ms.
Camera = v36 `licks_all.csv` (cam4 LP, including incomplete licks, `on_ms` from the cue). Scripts, CSVs and `summary.log`:
`M:/MICROSCOPE/Priya/Widefield/labcams/decoder_mix_checks/cue70_check_1104/` (`cue70_check.py`, `summarize.py`,
`examples.py`).

**Shape.** The pooled first-contact histogram, 10 ms bins, is flat from 0 to 80 ms (291, 343, 321, 300, 321, 311, 350,

## 2. Bout run-through, and does the ENL rule hold?

Of the 2277 trials whose first post-cue contact is < 70 ms, **38 (1.7 %) continue a bout** (gap from the last pre-cue
contact < 250 ms). 2229 have no contact in the 2 s before the cue. The median gap from the last pre-cue contact to the
cue is 2.2-2.7 s (PS95 7.6-8.1 s), which is the ENL minimum plus a little. The 70-120 ms trials show the same run-through
rate (2.2 %). So these contacts are isolated licks emitted just as the lick-free stretch ends. That fits licks timed to
the expected cue (anticipatory), or spontaneous licks.

**ENL** (`gui_config.json` timing `precue_min` / `precue_max` = 2000 / 3000 ms in every session; `enforce` = true). In
the GUI's own `lick_on` events, **0 trials have a lick inside 2 s before the cue**: the rule holds as the task applies
it. The DAQ channel sees some contacts the GUI did not register, mostly in the last 150 ms before the cue. Share of
trials with a DAQ contact within 2 s of the cue: PS92 pre 10.7 %, PS93 pre 8.6 %, PS94 pre 1.7 %, PS95 pre 0.3 %;
post-stroke 0.1-3.1 %. The cue-locked contact counts per 50 ms are: -500 .. -200 ms, ~16; -150 ms, 61; -100 and
-50 ms, 297 each; 0 ms, 1590; 50 ms, 2122. The step up at exactly the cue is what an ENL suppression would produce. The
GUI resets on detected contacts, so contacts before the cue are under-represented. After the cue nothing is
suppressed, and the background (anticipatory) rate shows unmasked until the response arrives at ~85 ms.
`table_enl_check_animal_epoch.csv`, `table_runthrough_animal_epoch.csv`.

## 3. Camera licks (cam4 LP, including incomplete)

Camera onsets in 0-70 ms per animal x epoch: PS92 205 / 67 / 45 / 202, PS93 158 / 36 / 55 / 213, PS94 104 / 29 / 46 /
72, PS95 28 / 6 / 51 / 267 (pre / acute / subacute / chronic). Of the 1584, 608 are in `licks_precue.csv` (peak
< 70 ms) and the rest are in `licks.csv`. The camera first-onset histogram (10 ms bins) has a dip at 50-60 ms (5)
between 30-50 ms (50, 32) and 60-70 ms (966). That dip is most likely a **seam artifact of the two passes** (not verified frame by frame). The pre-cue pass ends
at a peak of 70 ms and the post-cue pass starts at 70 ms, so a lick whose peak falls near 70 ms can be lost. The
60-70 ms onsets are real cue responses: tongue onset leads contact by ~20-40 ms. **So the camera floor is on the lick
PEAK (`t_ms`), and an onset-based 0-70 ms exclusion would cut ~1000 real camera responses.** Any change must keep the
camera rule on the peak, or move both camera passes together.

## 5. Reading

- 70 ms does not cut real cue responses. Below ~80 ms the first-contact distribution is a flat background, and its
  height follows each animal's pre-cue licking (PS92 > PS93 >> PS94 > PS95). The cue response begins at 80-90 ms.
- 50 ms is not better supported: 50-70 ms is the same background as 0-50 ms. 100 ms is worse: it would cut the start
  of the response (PS94/PS95: 5th percentile ~100-117 ms; pooled first contacts in 80-100 ms ~1100, mostly real). 80 ms would
  be equally defensible. It is not worth a rebuild: class changes at any floor from 0 to 80 ms are in single digits.
- Priya's proposal (0-70 ms belongs to neither pre-cue nor post-cue) fits the data. These contacts are spontaneous,
  but their rate is inflated by the ENL selection at the cue, so counting them as "pre-cue" would mix a selection
  effect into the pre-cue rate.
