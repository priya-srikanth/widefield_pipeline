## What the last few days have actually been about

The pipeline half is done and running nightly. The work since 8/19 is the POST-STROKE READOUT: not
"is decoding worse" (it is, and that was never in doubt) but **what changed about how the cortex
encodes where the tongue has to go**. One question, asked more and more carefully:

    Pre-stroke you can read spout position out of pre-cue activity.
    After the lesion the animal fails at the contraversive positions.
    Is the position CODE still there and unread, or has it changed?

The instrument is a **pairwise position axis**: for each of the 15 position pairs, the direction in
LocaNMF component space separating them, fitted pre-stroke, then compared with the post-stroke axis
for the same pair (`wfield_local/position_axes.py`). Three questions kept apart — is it different
(cosine), does a coherent axis still exist (its own split-half reliability), and is it the lesion
(the null).

**Most of the effort has gone into the null and the trial selection, not the measurement.** Four
nulls were tried and three were wrong; the outcome split was cutting the trials the wrong way for
the exact positions the lesion affects. Both stories are in DECISIONS.md under their 2026-08-23
headings, with the wrong turns kept, because the wrong turns are what a reader will otherwise repeat.

### What the outcome-blind arm found, and what it is allowed to say

Pooling every trial at a position regardless of outcome is what finally gives far_R usable cells
(0/15 before). Read against each animal's matched null:

- **CUE window** — three of four animals separate far_R (+0.27, +0.23, −0.06) from every other pair
  (+0.89, +0.52, +0.50). PS94's first block is −0.12: not rotated, *unrelated*.
- **PRE-CUE window** — only PS92 is position-specific (+0.35 far_R vs +0.78 the rest). In PS93, PS94
  and PS95 every pair moved by about the same amount, which is what a **global** change looks like,
  not a lateralised lesion effect.
- **Two recoveries, found independently.** PS95 pre-cue goes 0.59 (11/12 cells below null) → 0.96,
  reproducing the recovery first seen in the lick class from different trials. PS94 cue goes
  −0.12 → +0.38 → +0.60.

**The composition control is clean** (`scripts/axis_composition_null.py`): building the same
lick/no-lick asymmetry by hand inside pre-stroke leaves the axis where it was — +0.90 to +1.26
across three animals, and +0.98 for Priya's deliberately one-sided sated-tail arm. Outcome mixture
cannot manufacture the post-stroke values. One exception worth carrying: PS95's `far_R|far_L` in the
sated-tail arm is +0.52, the only cell below 0.8 in the whole control, and it is the pair the story
rests on.

**What is still uncontrolled is MOVEMENT, not composition.** The control lives in the pre-cue window,
which is lick-free on both sides by construction. The cue window is not: a pre-stroke far_R trial
contains a lick and a post-stroke one usually does not. So the pre-cue result is controlled and
mostly global; the sharper cue result is position-specific but confounded. **Do not merge them into
"the far_R code is lost"** — the two windows disagree, in the direction the confound predicts.
