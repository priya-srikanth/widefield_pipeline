Within a session: latency slows and the far landing pattern drifts ipsilesionally — separate from quitting

behavior

Question (Priya, 11-07). The first lick slows and the far-contra landing drifts toward the lesion side within post-stroke sessions. Is this the animals quitting, and is it fatigue, satiety or time?

  • Latency slows within every session, more after the stroke. Pre +61 ms/h (4/4); change vs own pre acute +126 ms/h [+37, +230], 4/4; subacute +57 (4/4, CI includes 0); chronic about 0.
  • The far landing pattern drifts ipsilesionally within the session, largest at far contra. Subacute far contra −0.20 mm/h [−0.36, −0.07], 4/4; far ipsi −0.16 (4/4); chronic far ipsi −0.17 (4/4). Peak tongue speed and contact barely change.
  • Not quitting. The drift is the same size with the last 15 min before the quit removed, aim shows no change before quits, and a session's drift does not predict when it ends. Latency does rise in the 10 min before a quit, but pre-stroke as much as after (+150 ms vs time-matched sessions, 3/3): a general sign of quitting, not of the stroke.
  • Paced like a clock, not like water. Within a session time, licks and water are collinear (r 0.96–1.00). Between sessions and animals the drift does not scale with how fast water is collected or with drop size. Satiety vs time is judged only from these comparisons (no manipulation).
  • Unit. Slopes per hour; per 100 trials gives the same sign in every animal. Neither depends on the session's trial rate. Post-stroke trials shorten only through the pre-cue (ENL) wait (acute −2.0 s, 3/4).
