    Behavioral states after the stroke

    VLS stroke, cohort 1 (PS92–PS95, lesion left: ipsi = ipsilesional = mouse left). Is the post-stroke change in engagement (GLM-HMM states over trials), in execution (outcome within an engaged state), or a reorganized movement repertoire (AR-HMM syllables, treadmill)? Every readout is the change from each animal's OWN August pre-stroke sessions. lick = behavior only (no imaging on this page). Code: wfield_local/behavior_states.py, figures scripts/states_page_figs.py. Findings: docs/FINDINGS.md section 9.

    GLM-HMM engagement states · FINDINGS 9.1

    1. Engagement states (GLM-HMM, K = 5) lick

    One GLM-HMM fit to every trial of every session (all animals, all epochs). Each state has its own multinomial GLM from the trial's inputs (intercept, spout, a "moved" flag; animal offsets shared across states; no previous-outcome input) to the trial's outputs (outcome class, pre-cue lick, ITI lick, running pre-cue and in the ITI). The states' roles come from their own profiles: engaged = ≥ 90 % of trials attempted (states 0, 1, 2; 2 = engaged while running), partial (state 3: half attempted), disengaged (state 4: no response .60, jaw-only .35).

    Disengagement rises acutely and subacutely and is gone by chronic; it is not spout-specific. Inside the engaged states, acute far-spout attempts continue but miss: the acute far-contra deficit is execution, not motivation.

    Model choice. Held-out McFadden pseudo-R² (4 interleaved session folds), total over outputs, K = 1–5: .108 / .340 / .396 / .444 / .470. K 3 → 4 gains in every fold (mostly running); K 4 → 5 in 2 of 4 folds; K = 5 chosen (Priya 11-17; states can be combined post hoc). K = 6–8 plateau test running. Table: glm_hmm/v1/select_k_pooled_all_v2.csv.

    Treadmill (DAQ wheel) · FINDINGS 9.2

    2. Treadmill push and walking around the cue lick

    Model-free, from the DAQ treadmill alone. Push = backward wheel speed above 0.5 mm/s in the cue window (−0.25 to +0.75 s); the same test 2 to 1 s before the cue (spout already in place) is the control. Walking = forward speed above 3 mm/s (the repo's running threshold). Thresholds: configs/defaults.yaml segmentation.push and segmentation.running.

    The push is a cue-locked task behavior (essentially absent outside the working period) that halves acutely and then grows past pre: chronic pushes are more frequent, faster and earlier. Walking around the cue does not change reliably.
    AR-HMM syllables · FINDINGS 9.3

    3. Movement syllables (AR-HMM) lick

    Sticky AR-HMM (jax-moseq; κ 5e7, PC-space dither 0.1, 24 states, 19 used) on 25 Hz face, tongue, jaw, FaceRhythm and treadmill features, fit on 31 sessions across animals and epochs and decoded on all 107. States are grouped by Priya's reel review (11-17; reels_pooled_all_s1/state_labels.csv): quiet 5-8-19, slow nose 1, chewing 21-22, ipsilesional licks 0-7-9-23, central licks 10-12-18, contralesional licks 2-15-20, push 11-13, slow walk 4, walk 6. Readouts use the GLM-HMM engaged trial cycles (previous trial's stop to this trial's stop).

    Acutely the engaged animal sits quiet more and makes fewer contralesional licks; behavior is more stereotyped. Chronically lick syllables shift toward ipsilesional and central and the cue push grows: a reorganized repertoire rather than a return to pre.

    Model checks. Seed stability (frame agreement after matching states by mean feature profile): pooled seeds .66 / .49, .82 / .80 with the quiet cluster 5-8-19 merged; pre-stroke-only seeds .82 / .64 (one stable quiet state). AR-HMM beats a Gaussian HMM on agreement with independent DAQ labels. Fits and provenance: arhmm/v1/fits/PROVENANCE.txt.

    TIM · FINDINGS 9.4

    4. Task-independent movement (TIM) lick

    Movement not explained by the task (after Yin et al. 2025), per session, in two frames: refit within the session, and frozen to the pre-stroke model.

    Unlike Yin et al., within-session TIM does not rise after the stroke: tongue TIM falls acutely in 3 of 4 animals (less spontaneous licking; PS95 rises). In the frozen frame the acute nose rise (4/4) is a contralesional posture shift of the nose (−2.1 / −0.8 / −5.1 / −4.5 px), not more variability. This fits the stiller acute state seen here (more quiet syllables) and in the quiet-event results.

    6. Methods and caveats

    • Sessions and baseline. 107 sessions with decoded syllables (GLM-HMM: 119); epochs from epochs.epoch_of; pre-stroke = August only (config.early_pre).
    • Statistics. Session markers open (shape = animal); pooled = mean with 95 % CI (nested bootstrap animals → sessions, analysis_kit.boot_ci). * = paired animal-weighted bootstrap of the change vs each animal's own pre (boot_delta) excludes 0; per-animal tables written beside each figure (*_per_animal.csv).
    • GLM-HMM emissions are shared across epochs, so per-state outcome rates are not stationary (Fig 1.3); the states are used as an engagement gate.
    • Near-mid jaw-only cannot be called (spout occludes the chin on cam4; cam1 failed review): no-lick near-mid trials count as no response.
    • AR-HMM reference fit = pooled_all seed 1 (local refit 11-17, same command as O2); groups from the reviewed state_labels.csv; changing a group there changes every readout here on the next collect.
    • Reproduce: python -m wfield_local.behavior_states collect then python scripts/states_page_figs.py.
