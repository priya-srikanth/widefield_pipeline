    Post-stroke cortical activity in state space: scaled, rotated, shifted or new?

    DRAFT, EXPLORATORY (11-07). A brain-computer-interface (BCI) framing of recovery: is the post-stroke population activity the pre-stroke activity scaled, rotated, shifted, re-associated or new, and how does it get there through the acute and subacute periods? VLS stroke (subcortical, no cortical tissue loss), PS92–PS95, lesion left (contra = right hemisphere = contralesional). 107 sessions (31 pre / 16 acute / 17 subacute / 43 chronic), 48.6k first-lick trials (contact + incomplete licks). August-only pre. Two alignments throughout, cue first, then lick: cue −0.5..2.0 s from the cue, and first lick −1.0..0.7 s around the first lick, which removes the long acute latencies (acute far-contra first lick median 0.78 s, 90th percentile 2.4 s after the cue). A claim needs ≥ 3/4 animals. neural = imaging. Plan, numbers and caveats: docs/STATE_SPACE_RECOVERY_PLAN.md (branch analysis/state-space-recovery-20261107). Click a figure to zoom.

Caveat: this mesoscale space is very low-dimensional — one cortex-wide mode carries 84–96 % of the mean response and 77–83 % of single-trial fluctuations (participation ratio 1.1–1.4 for the means, 1.4–1.7 for single trials; areas correlate |r| .75–.95); the spout-specific part has a participation ratio of 1.9–2.7. “Within the pre space” is therefore a statement about this coarse space, not about neural manifolds in the BCI sense.

No correction across readouts or epochs: exploratory. Some readouts reach * but not † (e.g. acute pattern r and amplitude, lick-aligned): read those as these four animals, not a population claim.

    2. What the readouts can and cannot detect neural

    Rotation: only ≥ ~20° in the main plane is reliably seen (≥ ~30° at 15 trials per spout). Region gains, especially a hemisphere-wide one, read as “outside the pre space” as strongly as a genuinely new dimension, so “new pattern” needs gains ruled out separately. A shared shift is weakly detected (.39 at half its norm). Amplitude inflates raw trial spread; the amplitude-free spread does not.
    Single trials, session vs session · plan “Single-trial run”

    3. What kind of change: larger, shifted, mostly inside the pre space neural

    Chronic, both alignments: amplitude +.38 / +.30 (cue / lick, 4/4), shared shift +28 / +29 noise units (4/4), a small out-of-space part beyond gains (+.006 / +.003, 4/4; the sim says region gains could produce this), pattern r −.10 / −.04 (0/4 back). Acute: cue-aligned the pattern looks strongly re-patterned (r −.20) and amplitude barely up (+.12); first-lick-aligned r is −.08 and amplitude +.22 (4/4): much of the acute “re-patterning” was the late acute responses falling outside the cue window. Response size grows at every spout (Figs 3.3–3.4; lick-evoked traces by epoch are on the goal page, Figs 14.7–14.22, FINDINGS 0b). Trial noise does not scale with the signal (Figs 3.7–3.8): chronically the response size² grows ~1.8× but baseline trial noise only ~1.2×, and acute / subacute trials vary more than their response size predicts. So trial spread is read RAW: single trials scatter more acutely (+.43 cue, 3/4) and a little more chronically (+.13, 4/4); dividing by amplitude² over-corrects. The raw picture is dominated by one cortex-wide mode (methods): once it is removed, or only the spout-specific part is kept (Fig 3.9), the post-stroke pattern is clearly re-patterned (chronic r −.11 to −.20, 0/4 back) with 5–10× more activity outside the pre space, robust to like-for-like trial selection. “Mostly within the pre space, gain + shift” describes the cortex-wide mode; the spout-specific code is reorganized.

    4. It is not the changed movement, except the acute “rotation” neural

    Pattern change, amplitude, out-of-space part, shared shift and distance from pre hold on on-target trials and when trials are grouped by where the tongue landed, in both alignments. The acute rotation beyond gain does not (cue +.032 (4/4) → +.011 (2/4) on target / +.012 (3/4) by landing; lick +.014 (4/4) → +.006 / +.002): it came from the changed landings.

    Contact vs incomplete first licks. Primary = contact + incomplete (the working-trial definition); contact-only = robustness; incomplete-only = descriptive; plus a direct within-session contrast. Share of first licks that touched the spout: far contra .80 pre, .05 acute, .44 subacute, .71 chronic; other spouts .86–.95 in every epoch — the split matters mainly for acute / subacute far contra.

    Contact-only reproduces the primary result in both alignments (pattern change, larger amplitude — acutely even larger, +.31 cue / +.40 lick, 4/4 — out-of-space part, shared shift, position on the line); the acute rotation beyond gain is again n.s. Incomplete-only (template readouts only: pre sessions have too few incomplete licks per spout for session-pair readouts) lags on the recovery line: chronic position .64 / .72 (cue / lick) vs .95–.97 for all trials, chronic-like share +.22 vs +.40. Within the same session and spout, incomplete licks have a smaller response than contact licks, most of all acutely (lick −.64 log2 vs pre −.31; cue −.90 vs −.29), are less like the pre template (r −.09 acute vs −.04 pre), and sit less far along the pre → chronic line (chronic −.30 lick / −.51 cue): the shift toward the chronic state is carried mostly by contact licks.
    Balance at matched movement · FINDINGS 0b

    5. Hemispheric balance: a contralesional shift at contra-side targets that is not the movement neural

    With every trial matched on target AND lateral landing (cue window): subacute / chronic, the orofacial balance moves toward the contralesional hemisphere at contra-side and middle targets (far contra +.27 / +.35, 4/4; chronic close contra +.24, far mid +.12, close mid +.14, 4/4), but not at ipsi-side targets (chronic close ipsi −.11, far ipsi +.06). It is not one hemisphere gaining everywhere: the target-to-target spread of the balance compresses (chronic −.27, 0/4 up; consistent with FINDINGS 0b “~30 % flatter”). Acutely, ipsi-side targets go the other way (far ipsi −.39, close ipsi −.24, 0/4), and the far-contra rise (+.60 by target, 4/4) halves at matched landing (+.33, 2/4: PS94 / PS95 +.8, PS92 / PS93 negative). The averaged far-ring balance in the first-lick window (0.1–0.5 s after the lick) shows the same direction (+.31 / +.29 / +.29 by target, 4/4), smaller when grouped by landing (+.12 / +.09 / +.14).

    6. How it gets there along the pre → chronic line: the whole cloud slides neural

    On the pre → chronic line, acute sessions sit near pre (alpha −.13 cue / +.07 lick) while far from pre in other directions (section 7). Subacute moves most of the way (alpha .59 / .68, 4/4). The single-trial distributions move as a whole; chronic-like trials are not over-represented in the tails before the mean gets there (excess about .01 acute, .00 subacute in both alignments). No search-then-select signature at this resolution.
    State-space trajectories · across days and within the trial

    7. In two dimensions: an acute detour, then back toward the line; larger, shifted loops neural

    Across days: the acute change is mostly SIDEWAYS: 80–95 % of acute − pre is orthogonal to the pre → chronic direction in every animal and alignment. Acute sessions sit +.56 to +1.12 (lick) off the line, in units of the whole pre → chronic distance; subacute sessions are back to +.05 to +.37 while moving along it (+.42 to +.90); chronic is on the line. In PS94 the acute sessions walk back day by day. So the path is a detour (out, then in and along), not a straight slide from pre. Within the trial: post-stroke trials make larger loops (scaling), clearest at the close spouts. Against time (Fig 7.8) the close-spout difference is two things: a larger PC1 peak, and a PC2 transient 0.1–0.3 s after the lick that close-spout trials lacked before the stroke but far-mid / far-contra trials already had — post-stroke close-spout responses take on part of the far-spout shape. In the full 62-area space every spout’s response grows (Fig 3.4), so the close > far contrast is mostly how the change projects into this plane, not a close-only change. The 95 % bands are wide (4 animals): pre and post separate mainly at close ipsi / close mid. PC2 contrasts posterior-medial cortex (RSP, VISam, SSp-tr: +) with contralesional orofacial cortex (SSp-m, SSp-n, tjM1: −). Not explained by the lick: close-spout reach got longer (e.g. PS92 close ipsi 3.0 → 4.4 mm) but does not track the transient trial by trial (|ρ| ≤ .16); and not by the shorter post-stroke latencies (the cue response falling into the window): at matched latency < 150 ms the close-spout transient is 5 pre vs 44 subacute / 33 chronic (far spouts 3 → 16 / 13).     Confound · FINDINGS 8 (crossnobis drift)

    8. Part of the chronic state lies along the pre-stroke drift neural

    Pre-stroke sessions drift: +.18 / +.12 / +.10 / +.04 pre-pre units per day cue-aligned (+.18 / +.06 / +.10 / +.005 lick-aligned; PS92 / 93 / 94 / 95). In PS92 / 93 / 94 the drift direction points toward the chronic state (cosine +.59 / +.48 / +.63 cue, +.41 / +.41 / +.37 lick; 35 / 23 / 40 % and 17 / 17 / 14 % of chronic − pre along it) and the acute change does not (cue −.44 / −.36 / −.42; lick −.18 / −.01 / −.56). After the stroke the drift slows in those three (chronic +.026 / +.001 / +.006 cue, +.044 / −.002 / +.004 lick per day, over 25–39 days); PS95 barely drifted before and drifts slightly in chronic (+.020 / +.015), and its drift direction is unreliable. Whether chronic is partly “where practice was heading” needs a no-stroke reference.
