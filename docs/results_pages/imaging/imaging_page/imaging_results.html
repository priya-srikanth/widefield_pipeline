    Context · what the epochs mean

    0. The behavioural deficit that defines the epochs behaviour

    Accuracy falls at the far spouts, most at far contra, and recovers at animal-specific rates. Acute = days when far-contra accuracy is below 25 % of the animal's own pre-stroke level; chronic starts when that animal's hit rate flattens. Every neural result below is stratified by these per-animal epochs.

    Where the position code is in cortex · decoder weights as activation patterns

    14. Haufe decoder maps: far contra and far middle flip sign acutely after the cue, and return neuralcoupled reference

    A decoder weight is a filter, not a map: it can load heavily on a pixel that carries no signal, only to cancel shared noise (Haufe et al. 2014). The Haufe transform A = Cov(X) b turns each position’s weights into the activity pattern the decoder actually reads. Decoders are refit in every session on the SVT (full-resolution pixels, no component intermediary), DAQ licks only, no kinematics. The maps are one-vs-rest, so the six positions are coupled: read a far-contra drop and a near-position gain as one event. The reference-free response maps of section 7 are the deficit measure; these say where each position’s signal sits. Not the same maps as the encoding page’s Haufe figures (Figs 3.33–3.35): those decode continuous lick POSITION (lateral / sagittal, 3-D tongue) with a ridge, lick-locked (0–0.2 s), on each animal’s pre-stroke 200-component basis, refit per epoch; 3.35 is one-vs-rest per spout on cued licks, without the mean-reference coupling of these maps.

    After the cue, far contra and far middle lose their pre-stroke maps acutely and flip sign — their trials become less active than the average trial over both hemispheres (Fig 14.2; far contra in all four animals, Fig 14.8); far middle’s map no longer clears zero acutely (Fig 14.5). Both return toward their pre-stroke pattern by chronic. In the lick window the maps keep their pre-stroke layout whenever there are enough far-contra licks to fit one (Fig 14.3, 14.9). The pre-cue maps are too weak to localise (Fig 14.4) and their acute change does not replicate (Fig 14.7).
