## WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

### The REST baseline replaces "quiet"

`segmentation.quiet` excluded **8 s after every reward** — carried over from stroke_orofacial, whose
task runs on an 8 s post-tone window; ours has a 3.5 s response window. Worse, anchoring on REWARD
anchors on PERFORMANCE, so the category tracked the deficit.

**REST = between trials AND not running AND not licking.** Window: `cue + response_window + 0.5 s`
→ next **`trial_start`**.

| | retired | REST |
|---|---|---|
| acute/pre rest fraction | **3.89** | **1.11** |
| chronic median rest | 0.007 | **0.030** |
| rest bouts, cohort | 14,017 | **26,010** |
| bouts admitting a 1 s segment | 0.58 | **0.846** |
| bouts admitting 2 s | 0.17 | 0.194 |

Every parameter carries its measurement in `configs/defaults.yaml segmentation.rest`. The four
scripts that produced them are in `scripts/rest_migration/` with a README.

Key sub-findings:
* **`trial_start`, not the strobe** — firmware moves the spout and only THEN pulses the strobe, so
  the strobe fires AFTER the movement. `trial_start` precedes it on 16,607/16,607 trials by a median
  0.925 s, which IS the travel time.
* **Settle 0.5 s** — reward lands 6 ms after the cue, so the trial window already contains it; the
  settle is pure margin. 0.5 s leaves 2.02 s of rest per trial against 1.52 s at 1.0 s.
* **No reward term at all.**
* **The state decoder's 1 s window STANDS** — re-derived, not inherited: 2 s fits 19.4% of the new
  rest bouts against the 17% that rejected it originally, while 1 s fits 84.6% against 58%.
* **Chronic is still 0.51 of pre** and that is the LICK term (73.7% of samples excluded pre-stroke,
  82.5% chronic). Behaviour, not a definition artefact. The chronic caveat stays.

### Naming

"quiet" is retired as a word. `rest` / `_RESTref_` / `quiet_<tag>_rest/`; the old definition is named
**`reward8`**. **The filename carries the baseline** (`reference_tag`), so a figure built on the old
masks is called `_REWARD8ref_` and cannot be mistaken. Old masks are never deleted; retiring is one
config line (`segmentation.rest.variant`).

|---|---|---|---|

already correlate between animals at r ≈ 0.88, so a large r is this statistic's resting state.

### 2. MEAN's near-position "increases" are the coupling artefact, measured

Near Middle acute: **+0.655, p = 0.003 under MEAN**, absent under both uncoupled references
(p = 0.243, p = 0.190). Under the MEAN reference a high between-animal r is what coupling PREDICTS.

### 3. The chronic column is uninterpretable under EVERY reference

REST chronic: observed +0.43 to +0.72 against nulls of +0.50 to +0.51, every p > 0.2 — unchanged
despite estimating chronic from 4.3× more frames. **More frames did not fix it.**

### 4. A position-graded amplitude change, NOT yet established

Acute amplitude vs pre, REST: **1.48 / 1.21 / 1.27 (near) → 1.01 / 0.69 / 0.48 (far)** — monotone
across six independently-analysed positions. Only far-contra has a position-specific SHAPE.
**Nothing has tested the gradient itself**; the test that would is per-animal amplitude ratios plus
the nested bootstrap on the near-minus-far contrast.

### 5. REST "carries position information" — it does not; it is DRIFT

6/6 positions significant (up to 1,042/2,022 bins), ratio 1.45. But positions run in ~6-trial
BLOCKS, so position is confounded with time-in-session:

    DRIFT     same position, early vs late      RMS 0.00282
    POSITION  different positions, matched time RMS 0.00288    **ratio 1.02**

Drift aliased onto blocks, not position coding. **The reference is not conceptually broken** — but
it exposes the defect below. Figure: `epoch_15x_REST_by_position_by_animal.png`.

### 6. THE REMAINING DEFECT — flat vs time-local subtrahend

`position_reference_maps.session_quiet_svt` subtracts ONE SESSION MEAN, which is flat and cannot
remove drift. `locanmf_position_encoder._quiet_baseline` has ALWAYS used a time-local baseline.
Same quantity, two treatments; the encoder's is correct.
`session_rest_svt_timelocal` now implements it map-side (12 bins, median, interpolated; carries
SD 0.047 of across-time structure the flat mean is blind to). **NOT WIRED** — see TO-DO.

### 7. The pre-cue reference was SELF-SUBTRACTING on the pre-cue arm

Feature window `[cue−2 s, cue]`, baseline `[cue−1 s, cue]` — the baseline was the window's own second
half. Skipped now; 13 files moved to `retired_INVALID_PRECUEref_on_precue_arm/`.

### 8. Pre-cue window evidence is variant-mismatched

Three variants across the evidence and **none is production**: the withdrawn sweep (`zerophase`/ROI),
the 08-13 re-run (`strobedetrend`/ROI/16 sessions), and what we run (`meegkit_hpfit`/LocaNMF). The

## DECISIONS, AND WHY

* **Time-local rest baseline, applied to the SVT before `trial_features`** — a flat session mean
  cannot remove drift, and evaluating a time-local one on averaged maps would need per-trial frame
  indices. Subtracting from the signal references each trial at its own moment.
* **`session_raw_maps` returns a DICT** — the tuple reached eight elements with two map dicts and
  two count dicts adjacent; a transposition would have produced a complete, plausible, wrong figure.
* **Docked is a SUBSET of rest, never a replacement** — it constrains the spout, not the animal.
* **Per-position travel reconstruction, or none** — one constant would inject up to 0.33 s of
  position-dependent error into the window start, manufacturing the very effect under test.
* **A session whose clocks will not align DROPS OUT** rather than falling back to the loose window.
* **Deck**: 46 slides cut; H6 was NOT a leaf so eight slides re-anchored to H6b; G4/G4b/G6 to the
  appendix because their illegibility is GEOMETRIC (aspect 1:2.5–1:3.9 → 14–23% scale → ~1 pt text);
  pooled FEVE cut (slide 25, not 23 — the label was right and the number was the slip); H7d moved
  after H6d; state-decoder caption converted to `{{SELF: …}}` sidecar tokens.
* **`nightly_figs` preflight** — two dates with no LocaNMF fit failed a step and the guard then
  blocked the whole deck. The guard was right; the input was wrong.

## 2026-09-13 — why `8rc` shows COLUMN STRIPES and not an X, and the X is real

Priya, looking at `epoch_8rc_matrices_crossnobis_rowcentred_precue_working`: *"I feel like I remember

### The X is real, and the pre-cue arm is where it is weakest

Correlation between crossnobis distance and spout separation on the **raw** (`epoch_8`) matrix, PRE
panel, using the true **2 rings x 3 lateral positions** layout:

| arm | city-block separation | lateral only | ring (near/far) only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cue** | **+0.804** | +0.691 | +0.190 |
| **precue** | **+0.594** | +0.471 | +0.190 |

**THE GEOMETRY IS ALMOST ENTIRELY LATERAL.** Near-vs-far contributes r = +0.19 in both arms. Mean
crossnobis by lateral separation: 0 steps **1.06-1.17**, 1 step **1.08-1.15**, 2 steps
**1.83-1.90**. It is a STEP at ipsi-versus-contra, not a gradient — which is why nI and nC stripe:
they are the two lateral extremes, so each is 2 steps from the far side, and those are the only
large cells.

**A METHOD NOTE ON THE NUMBER ABOVE.** A first pass reported r = +0.28 / +0.33 using a 1-D position
ordering (nI, nM, nC, fI, fM, fC), which treats fI as ADJACENT to nC. That is not the rig geometry
and the number was wrong; on the real 2x3 layout it is +0.594 / +0.804. A separation metric is a
model of the apparatus, not a formality.

### Why row-centring buries it

Additive decomposition of the off-diagonal structure (pre-cue, PRE panel):

    ROW effect        0.47
    COLUMN effect     0.31     <-- row-centring CANNOT remove this
    residual          0.23

Row-centring subtracts row means, so the 47% row effect goes and the 31% column effect stays intact.
What survives visually is the nI/nC columns rather than the X. The raw matrix is essentially
SYMMETRIC — row means 1.86 / 0.82 / 1.75 / 1.12 / 1.14 / 1.22 against column means 1.77 / 0.99 /
1.69 / 1.15 / 1.10 / 1.21 — so this is not an asymmetry artefact. nI and nC genuinely are far from
everything, in both directions.

### `fI` looking dissimilar to `nI` is a row-centring artefact, and the useful kind to know about

Raw `d(fI, nI) = 1.32`, one of the SMALLER distances in the matrix and well below the
lateral-separation-2 mean of 1.90. But **fI's own row mean is 1.12**, because fI is close to
everything — so 1.32 sits ABOVE its own row mean and renders red.

**ROW-CENTRING MAKES A TIGHT ROW SHOW RED WHEREVER IT IS MERELY AVERAGE.** Nothing is wrong with
fI-nI; the normalisation is doing it. This is the general hazard of the panel and belongs in its
caption.

### What to read where

* **"Which position did it move TOWARD"** -> `8rc`, within an epoch.
* **"Is the geometry spatial"** -> the RAW `epoch_8`, and prefer the **cue** arm where the X is

## 2026-09-13 - ORDERING CORRECTION: `restw` comes BEFORE the rest redo

Priya, mid-task: *"before we redo the rest analysis - I think we want to use the weighted rest
average for this right?"* **Yes - and the argument for it is now stronger than when it was designed,
because of a finding made after it was designed.**

`restw` (position-weighted ITI average) was queued as TO-DO #4, AFTER the redo. That ordering is
wrong and would cost the redo twice.

**WHY THE WEIGHTING IS NOW A CORRECTNESS REQUIREMENT AND NOT A REFINEMENT.** Every rest period sits
between two trials, so each one is position-labelled, and **rest carries position information**
(observed/null 1.449 on the strict docked window, 39/42 sessions). It follows that an UNWEIGHTED
session rest average is dominated by whichever positions are over-represented in that session - so
**the subtrahend itself carries position**, and subtracting it partially cancels the very effect
under test, by an amount that varies session to session with block composition. Before finding 11
this was a tidiness argument. After it, an unweighted rest subtrahend is a biased one.

**THE TRAP THIS IS NOT.** Priya raised the alternative earlier the same day - *"or should we do
quiet per-position?"* - and the two must not be confused:

| | what it does | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **`restw`, position-WEIGHTED** | average rest WITHIN each position, then average those six with equal weight -> ONE subtrahend per session | **correct.** Removes composition bias; positions stay independent because they share a reference containing no net position signal |
| **per-position rest** | reference each position to ITS OWN rest | **self-subtraction.** The persistence trace is precisely a match between a position's trials and its own rest, so this removes the signal and reports null |

That distinction is the whole reason `restw` was specified as weighted rather than per-position, and
it is worth keeping written down because the per-position version is the more obvious-looking one.

**SO THE ORDER IS:** finish the `restw` builder -> register it in `REFERENCES` **in the same commit**
(recurrent issue 3) -> then the docked redo, once, on the weighted time-local baseline.

## 2026-09-13 - THE REST DEFINITION, settled by measurement: docked + sweep-binned + position-weighted

Four questions were raised in one thread and all four are now measured. The definition below
supersedes the per-position-per-time-bin `restw` committed earlier the same day, which was DEFECTIVE
for a reason Priya identified before it ever rendered.

### 1. Is the signal HIGH-PASS drift-corrected? NO -- but it IS polynomial-detrended, and
`hpfit` does not mean what it looks like

Priya: *"do we need time local still? isn't the SVD already locally-corrected"*.

`joint_basis._load_session` reads `hemo_meegkit_hpfit/SVTcorr.npy` (config.py:97-113; env
`WIDEFIELD_HEMO_VARIANT` unset; no silent fallback -- a missing dir RAISES). **The `hpfit` in the
name means a high-pass for the COEFFICIENT FIT, not a high-pass on the signal**, and in production
(`--no-refit-t`) even that is skipped because the stock `T` was already fitted on high-passed
traces. The only drift operator on the OUTPUT is an order-10 polynomial over the whole session:

| period | 60 s | 300 s | 600 s | 1200 s | 3000 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| retained | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.94 | 0.18 |

**50% cutoff ~43 minutes**, and that is DELIBERATE -- position blocks last 57-121 s and must not be
filtered (PREPROCESSING_DECISION.md). So drift on the 2.5-5 min bin scale is fully present.

**TIME-LOCAL IS KEPT**, and confirmed by two independent controls on 6-8 sessions:

| comparison | real | control | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| vs time-shuffle (bin SIZES preserved) | 0.226 | 0.029 | **7.8x**, 6/6 |
| vs POSITION control (bin position-MIX preserved) | 0.226 | 0.056 | **4.05x**, 6/6 |

The second control was added because lag-1 autocorrelation across bins came out at chance
(-0.137 real vs -0.070 shuffled, 2/6) and the block hypothesis had to be excluded. It is excluded:
the structure is 4x more than position composition explains.

**A DECISION RULE OF MINE WAS WRONG AND IS WITHDRAWN.** `timelocal_needed` pre-registered "ratio >1
but lag1 ~ shuffled = a sample-size effect, trust lag1". That is false. **Lag-1 only detects
structure SLOWER than the bin width**; structure AT the bin width appears as magnitude with no
autocorrelation, which is exactly what was observed. The position control, not lag-1, resolved this.

### 2. The task runs a PERMUTED 6-SWEEP, and that is the right binning

Priya: *"the task is designed such that it will sweep through all 6 positions ... then restarts. so
we could do it in increments of every 6 blocks?"* **Verified, 8 sessions, 686 blocks:**

* **94.5% of sweeps are exactly 6 blocks** (104/110)
* **same-position adjacent blocks 2.80%** against the permuted-sweep prediction of **1/36 = 2.78%**
  -- a sweep boundary is the ONLY place a repeat can occur. Two decimal places.
* **13.8 sweeps per session** (8-18) against `REST_BASELINE_BINS = 12`

So binning by SWEEP costs **no temporal resolution** and makes every bin contain each position once.

**WHY THAT MATTERS: the committed `restw` was interpolating across a third of the session.**
Per-position TIME-bin coverage measured over 30 position-sessions: **mean 66.1%, min 16.7%, fully
covered 2/30**, with **16/30 positions time-CLUSTERED** (|mean t - 0.5| > 0.12, max 0.390).
PS94_0827 position 0 held 229 frames in **2 of 12 bins**, mean time 0.890. Binning by sweep takes
coverage to **100% by construction**, and each position's one block per sweep yields 3-7 ITIs =
125-290 docked frames, which is enough for a stable median. **The estimate that was ill-posed
becomes well-posed purely by changing what a bin is.**

NB the cohort-level finding that block order is shuffled (finding 9, mean normalised index
0.50 +- 0.006) does NOT imply within-session uniformity, and was relied on implicitly. It does not
carry that weight.

### 3. Position-weighting is still required, because blocks are 4-8 trials

Priya: *"the position weighting is still important because each block can be 4-8 trials"*. Correct,
and this was briefly and wrongly demoted to an edge-case guard.

Sweep-binning guarantees each position is PRESENT once per bin. It does NOT guarantee equal rest
FRAMES: an 8-trial block yields ~7 within-block ITIs against ~3 for a 4-trial block. Measured
PRE-STROKE, where engagement is uniform so this is the block-length effect nearly alone:

    PS92_0606  1475 891 1044 1169 1527 1284   max/min 1.7x
    PS93_0606  2960 3225 2765 2675 4538 4108  max/min 1.7x

**THE TWO MECHANISMS ARE ORTHOGONAL AND MUTUALLY ENABLING.** Sweep-binning fixes PRESENCE (and so
makes the per-position estimate computable at all); position-weighting fixes WEIGHT.

### 4. The lick and treadmill buffers: DO NOT SHORTEN

Priya: *"we shouldn't need a lick buffer at all - the spout isn't close enough to lick in ITI"*.
**The premise is CONFIRMED -- 0 licks inside 1,141+ docked intervals.** But `lick_buffer_s` does not
exclude licks, it excludes the RESPONSE to licks just before, and those two come apart.

Scored against a `[2.0, 5.0]` reference with a NOISE FLOOR (see below), 6 sessions:

| candidate | rest gained | n marginal | corr | floor | EXCESS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[1.5, 4.0]` | +4.8% | 1837 | 0.797 | 0.998 | **-0.201** |
| `[1.0, 3.0]` | +7.8% | 2998 | 0.747 | 0.998 | **-0.251** |
| `[1.0, 2.0]` | +10.5% | 4002 | 0.716 | 0.998 | **-0.282** |

Monotonic, no plateau, large marginal sets -- every step of shortening from 5 s down admits frames
that score below floor.

**AND THAT PATTERN IS ITSELF THE REASON NOT TO SET THE BUFFER FROM THIS STATISTIC.** A real decay
time would show excess ~0 above it and clearly negative below. CONTINUOUS degradation from 5 s
downward, with no plateau anywhere, is the signature of a confound: the statistic partly tracks
DISTANCE FROM THE TRIAL rather than distance from a lick, because frames nearer the trial differ for
cue and spout-movement reasons. The docked window reduces that but cannot remove it -- the docked
interval abuts the trial. **This sweep structurally cannot separate lick-decay from trial-proximity,
so following it to its limit just buys the longest buffer offered.**

### DECISION: `lick_buffer_s = [1.0, 2.0]`, chosen on BIOLOGY and RETENTION, not on the sweep

Priya: *"[2, 5] is too long and not biologically plausible. I think we should use something that
makes reasonable sense and doesn't discard too much meaningful data. [1, 2]?"* Agreed, and the
framing matters more than the number:

* **2 s post-lick** covers GCaMP decay (~0.5-1.5 s) plus the haemodynamic lag, with margin. A 5 s
  buffer implies a 5 s process, and there is none.
* **1 s pre-lick** covers tongue protrusion beginning BEFORE contact, and lick-onset detection
  jitter. There is no decay running backwards, so it does not need to match the post side.
* **It recovers +10.5% of rest** over `[1, 3]`, at the point in the session where rest is scarcest.

**THIS IS A JUDGMENT, NOT A MEASURED OPTIMUM, AND MUST NOT BE CITED AS ONE.** The sweep is
confounded (above), so it is evidence that shortening BELOW ~1-2 s is harmful -- `[0.25, 0.25]`
scores -1.073 and `[0, 0]` -1.252 against a `[1, 3]` reference -- and it is NOT evidence about where
the optimum sits above that. The floor it establishes is real; the slope above it is not
interpretable.

**THE EXPERIMENT THAT WOULD SETTLE IT, if it is ever worth settling:** hold distance-from-trial
FIXED and vary distance-from-lick. Among docked frames at a matched latency from `trial_start`,
compare those near a preceding lick against those far from one. That separates the two terms this
sweep merges. Not run -- the biological argument is sufficient for a buffer value and the confound
does not threaten any downstream claim.

### THE METHOD LESSON, which cost two wrong answers in one afternoon

**A MEAN MAP OVER FEW FRAMES IS NOISE, AND SCORES LIKE CONTAMINATION.** The first buffer sweep
reported marginal-vs-rest correlations of -0.39 to -0.59 with RMS 300-440% and was read as gross
contamination. PS95_0817 produced those numbers from **137 marginal frames** -- which is simply what
REST ITSELF looks like at n=137. Two conclusions were drawn and both were wrong: first "the anchor
is doing the buffer's job, go to 0.25 s", then nearly the reverse.

**THE FIX IS A FLOOR:** subsample the COMMON rest frames to exactly the marginal set's size, score
it identically, and read only `EXCESS = corr - floor`. With that, the floor sits at 0.995-0.998 and
the signal is unambiguous. Results are additionally WEIGHTED BY MARGINAL SET SIZE so an 83-frame
session cannot outvote a 6,000-frame one.

**Recurrent issue 2 again, in a new costume**: state what the statistic looks like under BOTH
hypotheses before reading it. A control that cannot fail is worse than one that fails -- and a
statistic with no noise model is a control that fails at random.

### THE RESULTING DEFINITION

    REST  = between trials AND spout DOCKED (dock -> next trial_start, excluding travel)
            AND treadmill < 1 mm/s (buffer open) AND outside a [1, 2] s lick buffer;
            runs shorter than 0.5 s dropped, applied AFTER the intersection.
    BIN   = one SWEEP (~13.8/session), each covering all six positions once.
    rest  = time-local baseline over those frames, per sweep-bin.
    restw = per-position median within each sweep-bin, averaged over the six EQUALLY.

### 5. The trailing partial sweep: carry forward the PRECEDING sweep, and it needs no special case

Three candidates were considered for the final, incomplete sweep, where some positions are absent:

| | what it does | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| normalise to the previous sweep's position-weighted NORM | rescales the partial bin's magnitude | **rejected** -- fixes SCALE but not SHAPE. The missing positions differ in spatial PATTERN, not just level, so the partial bin stays tilted toward the positions it happens to contain |
| fill from SESSION-WIDE position offsets | each missing position contributes its whole-session level | **rejected.** Priya: *"session-wide ignores temporal drift"* -- exactly the drift the time-local construction exists to track, averaged away at the one point furthest from the session mean |
| fill from the PRECEDING SWEEP's offsets | each missing position contributes its most recent measured level | **ADOPTED** |

**AND IT REQUIRES NO SPECIAL CASE, which is the strongest argument for it.** With SWEEP bins, each
position has a series of per-sweep medians and the ONLY missing entries are in the trailing chunk.
Interpolating that series is what `_timelocal_from_mask` already does, and `np.interp` CLAMPS beyond
the last known point -- so extrapolation at the end IS "carry forward the preceding sweep". The
desired behaviour is the default behaviour; the trailing chunk stops being a branch.

THE COST, STATED: a preceding-sweep offset rests on ONE block of that position (~125-290 docked
frames) against a session-wide one's ~13.8 blocks, so it is roughly 3.7x noisier in SD. That is
accepted because the construction ALREADY uses per-sweep per-position medians as its primary
estimate in every other bin -- this introduces no new noise regime, it declines to give the last bin
different statistics from all the others.

### 6. OPEN AND IMPORTANT: sweep coverage is over BLOCKS, not over surviving FRAMES

Priya: *"the time-weighted by sweep will be affected by thrown-out ITIs due to running etc"*.
Correct, and it qualifies the guarantee in section 2.

**SWEEP-BINNING GUARANTEES EACH POSITION'S BLOCK OCCURS IN EVERY BIN. IT GUARANTEES NOTHING ABOUT
THE FRAMES THAT SURVIVE THE MASK.** The behavioural terms -- treadmill, lick buffer -- are applied
AFTER the task structure has done its balancing, and nothing makes their removal balanced across
positions. Two consequences:

1. A position's block can contribute ZERO usable frames in a sweep if the animal ran or licked
   through it. The gap-filling then fires MID-SESSION, not only at the trailing chunk, and the
   carry-forward rule of section 5 has to cover it.
2. **THE SERIOUS ONE: exclusion may be POSITION-CORRELATED AND EPOCH-DEPENDENT.** If post-stroke the
   animal licks more after far-contralateral trials -- extra attempts, frustration, consumption
   differences -- then far-contra ITIs are preferentially removed by the lick buffer, and the
   baseline's composition once again TRACKS THE DEFICIT. That is the exact failure mode that retired
   the 8 s post-reward definition on 2026-09-12, reintroduced one layer down.

**THE MEASUREMENT TO RUN BEFORE THE REDO** (not yet run): per-position SURVIVAL RATE through the
mask -- what fraction of each position's ITI samples reach the final rest mask -- broken down by
epoch. Position-flat survival means weighting handles the remainder. Survival that tracks the
deficit means the definition is still performance-coupled and needs a further fix.

**NOTE THE ASYMMETRY IN WHAT EACH MECHANISM CAN FIX.** Position-weighting repairs UNEQUAL frame
counts. It cannot repair a position with ZERO frames, and it cannot repair a position whose
surviving frames are a BIASED SAMPLE of its ITIs -- if only the calmest far-contra ITIs survive,
that position's baseline is estimated from an unrepresentative subset no matter how it is weighted.

STILL OPEN: the treadmill buffer value; the per-position survival measurement above.

### WHY "GOOD ENOUGH FOR THE DECODER" DOES NOT IMPLY "GOOD ENOUGH FOR THE BASELINE"

Priya: *"the polynomial detrending is doing the work right"*, then *"but shouldn't that be enough
for the 'rest' detrend too then?"* The inference is reasonable and it does not carry, for a reason
worth stating once:

* **A DECODER IS INVARIANT TO DRIFT.** Per-fold standardisation absorbs a shared additive offset,
  and the output is a DISCRIMINATION, never an amplitude. Drift is nuisance variance it ignores.
* **A MAP REFERENCE REPORTS AN AMPLITUDE**, so an error in the subtrahend lands in the number
  directly. And drift is **not common-mode across positions**: each position's trials cluster in its
  own ~6-trial blocks and therefore sample drift AT ITS OWN TIMES. A flat baseline subtracts one
  value from all six and leaves each carrying its own block-time residual.

**MEASURED, for the record**: the polynomial detrend (`meegkit_hpfit`, order 10) has a 50% cutoff at
~43 MINUTES -- deliberately, because position blocks last 57-121 s and must not be filtered.
Retention is 0.94-1.00 from 60 s to 20 min. So structure on the 2.5-5 min scale passes essentially
untouched, and `timelocal_needed` measures the residual on the POST-detrend signal at 7.8x a
size-preserving shuffle and 4.05x a position-composition control.

**ONE HONEST QUALIFICATION ON WHAT THAT RESIDUAL IS.** The position control rules out block
composition. It does NOT distinguish photobleaching from AROUSAL or slow state fluctuation. Both are
slow and non-task, and for a position-map claim both are wanted gone -- so the baseline does useful
work either way, but "drift" is a loose name for it.

### EXISTENCE IS NOT CONSEQUENCE -- and the dependency structure that follows

`timelocal_needed` shows the residual EXISTS. Nothing yet shows it CHANGES A CONCLUSION, and the
two were being conflated. That matters because almost everything built in this thread rests on
time-local being necessary:

| component | justified by | independent of time-local? |
|---|---|---|
| **position-weighting** | rest carries position (1.443, 4/4 animals); composition tracks the deficit post-stroke | **YES.** Applies to a flat baseline too. Survives regardless. |
| **time-local** | residual slow structure | **UNTESTED at the level of conclusions** |
| sweep-binning, trailing-chunk carry-forward, reach-back, the survival analysis | making a per-position time-local estimate possible at all | **NO -- contingent entirely.** If time-local goes, all of it goes. |

**`scripts/rest_migration/flat_vs_timelocal.py` IS THE TEST**, and it is nearly free: a FLAT
reference is `raw[q] - quiet_flat`, and subtraction commutes with averaging, so both baselines come
out of ONE `session_raw_maps` call with no second feature build. It compares the two claims the REST
reference actually carries -- per-position amplitude by epoch (the graded-deficit claim) and
between-animal agreement at far-contra (the cross-position null's observed side).

**IF THEY AGREE TO THE THIRD DECIMAL, THE SIMPLER DEFINITION WINS**: flat + position-weighted, with
no bin-coverage problem, no trailing-chunk rule, and the survival concern largely dissolved. That
would be the right outcome, not a disappointing one.

### DECISIONS TAKEN IN THIS THREAD

* **`lick_buffer_s = [1.0, 2.0]`** -- on BIOLOGY and RETENTION, explicitly NOT on the sweep, which
  is confounded (it degrades monotonically from 5 s with no plateau, the signature of tracking
  distance-from-TRIAL rather than distance-from-lick). The sweep is good evidence that shortening
  below ~1-2 s is harmful (`[0,0]` scores -1.252 excess) and no evidence about the optimum above it.
* **`treadmill_buffer_s = [1.0, 2.0]`**, matching lick. Priya: *"we can do [1, 2] like lick - unless
  you think [0, 0] is defensible"*. **`[0,0]` is NOT defensible**: locomotion's haemodynamic tail is
  at least as slow as licking's, and at a 1 mm/s threshold the crossings are brief transitions --
  exactly where movement-related activity persists but the threshold has stopped flagging it. The
  single session showing corr +0.94 at `[0,0]` is one session, and single-session marginal
  statistics misled this analysis twice in one afternoon. Matching lick also gives the two terms ONE
  shared rationale instead of two.
* **THE DOCKED FROZEN DECODER USES THE SETTLED REST DEFINITION**, not the raw docked window
  (Priya: *"I want that to be whatever we settle on as the 'rest'"*). This resolves the open
  question in that arm's spec -- it is REST-GATED, not ungated.

  **TWO CONSEQUENCES THAT MUST BE CARRIED INTO THE IMPLEMENTATION.** First, the window becomes
  VARIABLE LENGTH per trial, since surviving frames differ, so fixed sub-bins do not apply: the
  natural form is the mean over surviving docked-rest frames with a minimum frame count, trials
  below it dropped AND COUNTED. At ~1.35 s, and given binning earned nothing pre-cue, losing
  sub-bins costs little. Second, and more seriously, **this decoder is the FIRST to inherit the rest
  mask, and therefore its survival bias.** If far-contra trials lose more frames post-stroke, that
  class gets fewer and less representative samples, which reads as a decoding deficit -- the exact
  claim the arm exists to make. The per-position survival measurement is therefore a PRECONDITION
  for this result, not hygiene for a baseline.

* **MISSING POSITIONS IN A SWEEP BIN: substitute from the nearest AVAILABLE neighbouring sweep**
  (Priya: *"if the last position 5 block was totally excluded, instead include the position 5 block
  before that"*), with two refinements. MID-SERIES, INTERPOLATE rather than only reaching backward --
  with data on both sides, using only the earlier one discards half the information and biases the
  estimate toward the past; nearest-preceding is correct only at the END, where there is no later
  value. And **BOUND THE REACH**: an unbounded search defeats time-locality, since a value five
  sweeps back (~10 min) has become the session-wide offset already rejected for that reason. Past
  ~2-3 sweeps, DROP the position from that bin and weight equally over those remaining, with the
  drop counted and reported -- a bin averaged over four positions must never be presented as one
  averaged over six.

  **AND SUBSTITUTION FIXES MISSINGNESS, NOT BIAS.** A position that always has SOME surviving ITIs
  which are an unrepresentative subset of its ITIs -- if only the calmest far-contra intervals clear
  the lick criterion post-stroke -- is invisible to any substitution rule, because nothing is ever
  missing. Only the survival measurement detects that.

## 2026-09-14 - PER-POSITION SURVIVAL: the predicted failure does NOT occur, and a different one does

`scripts/rest_migration/position_survival.py`, 32 sessions. The test asked whether the behavioural
exclusions remove ITIs evenly across positions, or whether the rest baseline's composition tracks
the deficit -- the failure that retired the 8 s post-reward definition, reintroduced one layer down.

**THE PREDICTION WAS STATED IN ADVANCE AND IS REFUTED.** The worry was that post-stroke the animal
would lick more after far-contralateral trials, so far-contra ITIs would be preferentially removed
by the lick buffer, starving that position of frames and manufacturing a deficit.

| epoch | p0 | p1 | p2 | p3 | p4 | **p5 far-contra** | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre | 28.3% | 24.4% | 27.6% | 38.1% | 35.6% | **56.7%** | **32.3%** |
| acute | 48.4% | 51.6% | 55.2% | 63.5% | 62.6% | **67.6%** | **19.2%** |

**FAR-CONTRA HAS THE HIGHEST SURVIVAL, NOT THE LOWEST, AND THE IMBALANCE SHRINKS POST-STROKE.**
The mechanism is performance-coupling running the OTHER WAY: far-contra is the hardest position, so
fewer hits -> less reward -> less consumption licking -> MORE of its ITI clears the lick buffer.
Acutely the animal licks less everywhere, survival rises everywhere (~35% -> ~58%) and the spread
compresses.

### WHAT IT DID FIND, AND IT JUSTIFIES POSITION-WEIGHTING INDEPENDENTLY

* **Survival is strongly position-dependent PRE-STROKE**: 32.3% spread, far-contra 56.7% against
  24.4% at p1 -- a **2.3x** difference in how much each position contributes to an unweighted rest
  baseline, in healthy animals with no deficit to explain it.
* **The spread is EPOCH-DEPENDENT** (32.3% -> 19.2%), so an unweighted baseline's composition
  changes with epoch even though its DEFINITION does not.
* **The lick buffer is the route.** Removing it drops the pre spread 32.3% -> 22.0% and lifts
  survival to ~66%; the treadmill term barely moves it (31.5% pre).

**THIS IS THE STRONGEST JUSTIFICATION YET FOR `restw`, AND IT IS INDEPENDENT OF EVERYTHING ELSE.**
It does not depend on time-local being necessary, on sweep-binning, or on the docked window. An
unweighted rest baseline is composed 2.3x more from far-contra than from p1, and that ratio moves
with epoch. Equal position weighting removes exactly that.

### TWO LIMITS, STATED

1. **ONLY PRE AND ACUTE ARE REPRESENTED.** The run sampled every 2nd session and subacute/chronic
   did not appear in the summary. A full-cohort run is going; until it lands, nothing here should be
   read as an epoch TREND beyond pre-vs-acute.
2. **WEIGHTING REPAIRS COUNTS, NOT SAMPLES.** If the far-contra ITIs that survive are systematically
   the POST-MISS ones -- no reward, no consumption licking -- then they are a different behavioural
   population, not merely a smaller one, and no weighting fixes that. Detecting it needs the
   surviving ITIs split by trial outcome, which is not run. **This is the residual risk and it
   should be stated wherever the REST reference carries an amplitude claim.**

## 2026-09-14 - The rest baseline is ENGAGED-PERIOD ONLY, and a polarity bug caught by counting

Priya: *"no splitting per outcome for now - but we do want to use the engaged session time for the
baseline (during working periods)"*.

### WHY, and it is the same failure a third time

The terminal sated tail is a DIFFERENT BEHAVIOURAL STATE, and crucially **how much of it there is
varies with epoch** -- a post-stroke animal disengages earlier. So an all-session rest baseline has
its COMPOSITION track engagement, which is the same performance-coupling that retired the 8 s
post-reward definition (route 1) and that `restw` corrects for position (route 2). This is route 3.
It also concentrates at the END of the session, where drift is largest.

**THE DECODE SCRIPTS ALREADY EXCLUDED IT; THE MASK NEVER DID.** `rest_position_decode` drops the
quit period explicitly and says why. So until now the baseline and the analyses built on it
disagreed about which part of the session counted -- the exact class of silent disagreement this
migration exists to end.

**IMPLEMENTED WITH THE SHARED GATE**, `precue_engagement_states.engagement_gate`, which is what
`beta_maps._quit_mask` and every `working`-class family already use. The rest baseline must not
acquire a second definition of engagement. BOTH bracketing trials of a rest period must be engaged,
not only the labelling one: a period whose FOLLOWING trial is already in the quit period sits on the
boundary of the state change.

### THE BUG, worth recording because of HOW it was caught

**`engagement_gate` RETURNS TRUE FOR *NOT*-ENGAGED.** `beta_maps._quit_mask` binds it as `ne` and
returns it as the QUIT mask. The first implementation here took it as `engaged` and inverted the
whole session: **527 of 554 trials on PS93_0818 were flagged as quit period**, and every baseline
would have been built from ~5% of the session -- the thin, late, unrepresentative 5%.

**IT RAISED NOTHING. IT PRODUCED A PLAUSIBLE BASELINE.** `restw` simply reported "only 3 positions
with a usable rest baseline" and dropped the column, which reads exactly like a thin session. The
only reason it was caught is that the gate PRINTS WHAT IT REMOVES, and 527/554 is impossible on its
face. After the fix: 27/554, and all six positions return.

**-> A GATE MUST COUNT WHAT IT REMOVES, OUT LOUD.** This is recurrent issue 1 ("a clean exit is not
a result") with a new instance, and it is the fourth time in two days that a COUNT, not an
exception, was the thing that caught a silent inversion.

### CONSEQUENCE FOR THE REDO

Rest frames per session drop by roughly the size of each session's quit period, which is
epoch-dependent BY CONSTRUCTION -- that is the point. Sessions whose engaged period is short may now
fall below `MIN_FRAMES_PER_POSITION` (200) at some positions, or below
`MIN_POSITIONS_FOR_WEIGHTED` (4) entirely, and will lose their RESTW column rather than contribute a
differently-composed baseline. **Those drops must be counted and reported per epoch**, because a
post-stroke epoch losing more sessions than pre is itself a result about the data, not a nuisance.

## 2026-09-14 — THE RESIDUAL SLOW STRUCTURE IS **NOT** PHOTOBLEACHING, and both channels are detrended

Two questions came up after the rest definition was settled, both about whether the drift handling
upstream is adequate. **Neither changes anything; both are recorded so they are not re-opened.**

### 1. Is the residual drift photobleaching? **NO — and the evidence was already in hand.**

Photobleaching is the obvious candidate for slow drift in widefield, and the order-10 robust
polynomial is precisely the right tool for it: bleaching is a smooth, monotonic, session-scale decay,
and a ~43 min 50% cutoff removes it while leaving the 57–121 s position-block band untouched
(retention 0.98–1.00). **That part is well-tuned and needs no change.**

But the residual structure `timelocal_needed.py` measured at the 2.5–5 min scale **has the wrong
shape to be bleach**. Bleaching is smooth and monotonic, so after centring it would show a **high
lag-1 autocorrelation across bins (~+0.7)**. Measured: **−0.065, against a shuffle of −0.081.** No
smoothness whatever — large, non-monotonic, uncorrelated bin-to-bin jumps.

| slow component | status |
|---|---|
| photobleaching | **REMOVED** by the order-10 polynomial, correctly tuned |
| residual 2.5–5 min structure | **NOT bleach** (lag-1 ≈ 0); arousal/state, or uncorrected hemo |
| effect on any MAP conclusion | **NONE** — `flat_vs_timelocal.py` |

**This retires the "uncorrected slow hemodynamics" alarm raised on 09-14 as speculation voiced
without checking the shape first.** The lag-1 number was already computed; it rules out the simplest
explanation. It does not identify what remains — but it does say the detrending is doing its job on
the thing it exists to do. **No preprocessing change. The `causal` variant becomes an optional
question, not a correction.**

### 2. Are BOTH 470 and 415 drift-corrected, or only one? **BOTH, symmetrically, at every stage.**

Asked because a one-sided detrend would be a real defect. It is not what the code does:

| stage | 470 (`a`, functional) | 415 (`b`, isosbestic) |
|---|---|---|
| SVD upstream | no detrend (`detrend_order=0`, verified in `local_wfield_summary.json`) | same |
| drift removal for the OUTPUT | meegkit order-10, masked (`hemo_variants.py:218`) | **same operator, same mask** (`:219`) |
| high-pass for the COEFFICIENT FIT | 0.1 Hz filtfilt (`:216`) | **same call, same line** |
| stock pipeline (`zerophase`) | acausal 0.1 Hz high-pass | **same** — the module docstring says "applied to both channels" |

`c = a - T @ b` (`:242`) therefore subtracts two identically-treated channels. The ONLY asymmetry in
the routine is `_finish` (`:203-207`), a **14 Hz LOWPASS on the violet only** — that is
`wfield.hemodynamic_correction`'s own behaviour (smooth the noisier isosbestic before regressing it),
and it is not a drift operator; it does not touch the slow band.

**THE SYMMETRY IS LOAD-BEARING, NOT COSMETIC — and the reason is photobleaching.** Detrending both
channels separately is NOT equivalent to detrending the corrected output:

* Bleach rates **differ** between 470 and 415 (different excitation energy, different photophysics),
  so the two channels decay along **different trajectories**.
* `rcoeffs` is **ONE SCALAR PER PIXEL**. It can SCALE the violet; it cannot RESHAPE a decay whose
  time course differs from the blue's. A bleach mismatch is something the regression **structurally
  cannot remove**.
* Detrending each channel against its own fit removes each channel's own bleach first, leaving the
  regression only the genuinely shared vascular component.
* meegkit's robust fit iterates its weights **per channel**, so the two detrend operators are not even
  identical as operators — which is exactly what lets two different bleach trajectories come out
  independently.

Had only the output been detrended, `T` would have been fitted on bleach-dominated traces and biased
toward whichever channel bleached harder. **Do not "simplify" this to a single detrend of the
corrected output.**

### WHAT REMAINS OPEN

**The ENCODER arm — and for a reason independent of what the residual IS.** FEVE is depressed by any
structured unexplained variance in the response, whatever its source. So the encoder drift arm still
wants measuring; it is now a question about **explained variance**, not about a preprocessing defect.
The flat MAP result still does not license simplifying the encoder (see the 09-14 handoff).

## 2026-09-14 (later) — WHAT THE LocaNMF/ROI ARM ACTUALLY READS, and the encoder time-local is WITHDRAWN

Verified against the 106 on-disk variant manifests, not against docstrings
(`scripts/rest_migration/what_locanmf_reads.py`, `variant_manifest_survey.py`).

### The read path, end to end

`defaults.yaml hemo.variant: meegkit_hpfit` -> `config.svtcorr_path()` ->
`wfield_local_results/hemo_meegkit_hpfit/SVTcorr.npy`. **The bare `SVTcorr.npy` exists on every
session and NOTHING READS IT as data** -- every consumer goes through `config.svtcorr_path` /
`svtcorr_in`. LocaNMF is fitted to the variant file (`locanmf_affine8v1_hemo_meegkit_hpfit`) and the
Allen-ROI arm is `mean_pixels(U_region) @` the SAME file, so **both arms see identical drift
handling** and the ROI-vs-LocaNMF comparison is not confounded by preprocessing.

**BUT THE BARE PRODUCT'S `T.npy`/`rcoeffs.npy` ARE USED.** Production builds with `--no-refit-t`
(`preprocess.py:631`), confirmed by `refit_t=False` in **106/106 manifests**. So the 0.1 Hz
Butterworth survives in the analysed data **ONLY AS `T`**, a (K,K) mixing matrix, and never touches a
trace. That is what "high-pass for the FIT, not the signal" means, stated precisely.

### THE DRIFT FIT IS ANCHORED TO THE ITI

`MASK_SPEC["strobedetrend"]` is `pre_s=0.25, from_strobe=True, require_quiet=False`, so `fit_mask`
removes **whole trials** (strobe-0.25 s -> cue+4 s) and the quiet step is SKIPPED. What the polynomial
is allowed to see is therefore essentially **the inter-trial interval**: median **24.2%** of frames
(min 3.05%, max 32.3%; the five lowest are early-June sessions already outside the curated set, and
the lowest CURATED session is 9.95%). No session is below 2%, and ITIs are spread uniformly through a
session, so 11 free parameters against thousands of well-distributed samples is comfortable.

**Consequence worth holding onto: the drift correction is fitted on the same epochs `rest` is drawn
from.** The signal is, by construction, flattened on the inter-trial baseline.

### THE ENCODER TIME-LOCAL IS WITHDRAWN -- this SUPERSEDES the 09-14 handoff's "do not fix this"

The handoff recorded, loudly, that time-local must be KEPT for the encoder: a regression intercept
absorbs a FLAT baseline error entirely but not a TIME-VARYING one, which depresses FEVE. **That
reasoning is sound in the abstract and skips a precondition.** Priya: *"if signal is appropriately
drift-corrected, then encoder arm should not need to be further locally-corrected."*

**A TIME-LOCAL BASELINE CAN ONLY REMOVE STRUCTURE THAT IS LOCALLY COHERENT.** It estimates a bin's
baseline from that bin's own frames and assumes the estimate predicts its neighbourhood. The lag-1
measurement says it does not: **-0.065 against a shuffle of -0.081** across 2.5-5 min bins. A bin's
estimate carries no information about the adjacent bin, so subtracting it removes no trend and
injects its own estimation noise. **At that window time-local is a NOISE INJECTION, and it would
DEPRESS FEVE for exactly the reason it was invoked to prevent.**

**LIMIT, stated rather than buried:** measured at 2.5-5 min bins only. A much shorter window
(30-60 s) is UNMEASURED and the residual could be coherent there. The encoder's time-local as
specified sits at the measured scale, so as specified it is falsified; a short-window variant would
need its own lag-1 measurement before being adopted.

**The encoder arm therefore changes from a DESIGN DECISION to a CONFIRMATION:** measure whether FEVE
improves when time-local is dropped. The expected answer is yes or no-change; a large drop would mean
the lag-1 result does not transfer from bins to trials and would reopen this.

### METHOD NOTE

The "do not fix this" instruction was written with more force than its evidence supported, and the
measurement that undercuts it was already in hand when it was written. **An instruction recorded
emphatically is not thereby better supported** -- and emphasis is what makes a wrong one durable.
