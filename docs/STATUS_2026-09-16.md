**THE REST POSITION CODE IS NOT THE TASK POSITION CODE.** `15s` finds the task map and that

window does not (flat against its null at every epoch). The block-boundary test says which way that
shared component points — rest resembles **the position just licked at**, not the one coming next

the next: **a real constraint on reading it as anticipatory**, and consistent with this project's
standing refusal to call it "a maintained motor plan".

### 6. SUBACUTE: the non-replication was the BIN (2026-09-17)

**The claim being withdrawn:** "subacute between-animal agreement is ~0 (0.030 flatpool / 0.052
restw) against acute 0.810, so the acute far-contra drop replicates and the subacute one does not."

**Why it was never a fair comparison.** `acute` is the first `acute_fraction` of post-stroke days;
`chronic` starts when THAT ANIMAL's hit rate flattens. **Subacute is neither — it is the
RESIDUAL**, and a residual bin has no common meaning across animals:

| animal | acute days | subacute days | median subacute day | n per bin (acute/sub) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS92 | 1–5 | 7, 9 | 8 | 5 / 2 |
| PS93 | 1–4 | 5, 7, 9 | 7 | 4 / 3 |
| PS94 | 1–5, 7 | 9, 11, 15, 18, 22, 25, 29 | **18** | 6 / 7 |
| PS95 | 1 | 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9 | **4.5** | 1 / 6 |

PS94's subacute IS the other animals' chronic day set; PS95's starts at day 2, inside the others'
acute range. **Acute replicates because it is ALIGNED — every animal starts at day 1 — not because
acute is biologically special.**

**The test.** `scripts/rest_migration/subacute_daymatched.py` re-bins the SAME maps by post-stroke
DAY, with the identical statistic and null. The day windows come out exactly balanced (6/6/6/6
sessions per animal for `d1-7`, 3/3/3/3 for `d5-9`, 2/2/2/2 for `d7-9`) where the epoch bins are
5/4/6/1 and 2/3/7/6. Far-contralateral, `restw`:

| binning | acute | subacute |
|---|---|---|
| epoch (aligned vs residual) | 0.822, p < 0.001 | **0.087, p = 0.48** |
| day-matched `d1-7` vs `d5-9` | 0.583, p = 0.0015 | **0.389, p = 0.034** |
| day-matched, `d7-9` (n=2 each) | — | 0.170, p = 0.22 |

**Subacute far-contra goes from at-null to significant.** Note that day-binning simultaneously
HURTS acute (0.822 → 0.583): the two binnings trade off, which is what a binning effect looks like
and what a biological one does not.

**THE POSITIVE CONTROL PASSES EXACTLY, which is what makes the subacute number usable.** PS92,
PS93 and PS95 all have `chronic_from = 11`, so for them the day window `d11-29` and the epoch bin
`chronic` select the SAME sessions — and re-binning must therefore reproduce the epoch result. It
does, across all 12 cells (2 references x 6 positions), **largest disagreement 0.000000**:

| | epoch-binned | day-binned `d11-29` [PS94 excl] |
|---|---|---|
| restw far_R | 0.4057 | 0.4057 |
| restw far_L | 0.6766 | 0.6766 |
| rest far_R | 0.4268 | 0.4268 |

So the re-binning machinery introduces nothing of its own, and **the subacute shift from 0.087 to
0.389 is attributable to BIN COMPOSITION alone.** Without this control the day-matched subacute
number would be indistinguishable from a coding difference between the two scripts.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH, and the limitation is symmetric.** A day window matches the
CALENDAR, not the recovery STATE — day 5 is acute for PS94 and subacute for PS93, and the animals
recover at very different rates (PS95 is at 0.83 of baseline by day 2; PS94 is at 0.14 on day 7).
So neither binning is correct: **epoch bins match state but make subacute a residual; day bins are
balanced but mix states.** The honest conclusion is that the subacute result is BINNING-DEPENDENT,
not that subacute replicates.

**THE RULE THIS SETS.**
1. **Never compare the residual subacute bin BETWEEN animals** — that is an aligned-vs-residual
   comparison, and the gap it produces is mostly bookkeeping.
2. A subacute claim must either use a **balanced day window and say so**, or be made **per animal**.
3. **PS94 is reported separately** in any epoch-binned analysis: it has no chronic bin and its
   subacute spans the others' chronic.
